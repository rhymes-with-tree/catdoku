# Catdoku

A family of cat-themed puzzle games, each a single HTML page. The first, Catdoku (in `catdoku/`), is a logic puzzle: place one 🐱 in every row, every column, and every color region — and no two cats may touch, not even diagonally.

## Play

Open `index.html` in any modern browser: the front page has a tile for every game, with an icon drawn from its board, a line about it (on wider screens; a phone shows three small tiles across, just the picture and the name), how many you've solved on this device once you've played, and a New badge on the newest games (added in the last week, at most four). The tiles are in one grid, shelf by shelf, each shelf starting with a label tile in its colour and running straight on after the last, with every game's tile in its shelf's colour; the shelves are by kind (Cat Logic for logic puzzles, Cat Math for numbers, Cat Trails for lines and paths, Cat Spells for words, Curious Cat for brain teasers, Kitten Play for quick games and Cat Travels for countries and maps), after today's challenge (a wide tile at the start of the grid) and a Favorites label with the games you've starred on this device (every tile has a ☆, and each game's page has a ☆ at the far right of its title, beside Games; the label only shows once you've starred one, and until then a line under the search box says how). A row of shortcuts under the search box jumps to each shelf, and the search box narrows them by name, line, shelf (by its name or a plain one, like "logic" or "words") or the puzzle a game is based on ("minesweeper" finds Hide & Seek). Every game's All games menu has the same search box, Favorites row and shelves (in the same shelf colours, from `G.SHELF_COLOUR` in `games.js`), with a star on every tile. The shelves, search words and favorites are kept in `games.js` (`G.GAMES`, `G.GROUPS`, `G.favorites`, `G.favStar`). No build step or dependencies. (Catdoku used to be the front page; old share links to it are passed on to `catdoku/`.) The icons are in `icons/`, drawn by `tools/make-icons.py` (`python3 tools/make-icons.py` draws them again).

## Across every game

- **Today's challenge:** one game a day is featured for everyone, at the top of the front page, at the top of every game's menu, and as a badge on that game's page. Winning any puzzle in it that day (in Pawlette, clearing 3 lines in one Swipe game, enough to bring in the third colour) does the challenge, and the win card says so ("Today's challenge solved! 4-day streak!"), with the streak of days in a row kept on this device. Before then the front page asks "Can you solve one today?" (or "Solve one today for a 4-day streak"). The games come round in cycles from 2026-10-18: the first starts with Catdoku and then takes the rest in a shuffled order, each later cycle is shuffled afresh, and the same game never comes up two days running. A new game, given an added date in `G.GAMES`, joins the first cycle that starts at least 3 days after it was added, so it has a few days on the site first (see `daily` in `games.js`).
- **How to play:** the first visit plays a walkthrough on the real board (everything else dims and a short line says what to look at). The How to play button plays the walkthrough again at any point in a puzzle, and leaves your progress as it was: Copycat and Unravel then point at your own boxes or strand rather than opening boxes or drawing a way through, Tail Chase and Bird Watch pause the run first, and Pounce, Bubbles, Toy Box and Twirl wait for anything moving or loading to settle. There's no rules card. If the puzzle is finished, it says to start a new one first (New puzzle, or Restart where there's no New button). Pawlette's Swipe mode has a walkthrough of its own. The rules aren't printed on the page; Pawlette keeps its colour wheel and Catwalk its direction tiles.
- **Layout:** status line, board (with any key or number pad that goes with it), action buttons, then settings (level or size, New puzzle) below the board. The action buttons are icons with their word small underneath, as in Toydoku (a lightbulb for Hint, a trophy for Best times, a ? for How to play…; `toolify` in `games.js` picks each by its id); The helpers of Toy Box, Bubbles and Bird Watch are tools too, with their uses left in a badge on the icon's corner, and Keyboard Cat's three hints are switches that turn green when on. Every New button and Catlas's Go keep their own look with a + or an arrow before the word, and the Level (or Size, or Round) picker shows a step icon in place of its word. A game's title is centred, with its name, cat and stripe, and Games (the menu) and the game's ☆ stand side by side at the far right (on the narrowest screens Games is its icon alone); a long name gets a little smaller rather than going onto two lines, unless even that won't fit. The action buttons go in one order: Hint first, then Undo and Restart, then the game's own buttons, then Share, Toys, Best times and How to play. Toy Box, Bubbles and Bird Watch put their helpers in a row of their own above. Toydoku is laid out like Sudoku.com instead: four icon tools under the board (Undo, Erase, Pencil with an ON/OFF badge, Hint, and Undo guess while there's a guess), one row of plain toys or numbers, then its other buttons in a row below. Every start-again button says Restart, and size pickers say Size. A switch or setting has a short line under it saying what it does. Keyboard Cat keeps its word list beside (or under) the keyboard, and Pawlette keeps its Zen board settings in a menu that opens.
- **Gold paw:** a perfect clear shows a gold paw on the win card: no life lost and no hint or Audit in Catdoku; no life lost in Hide & Seek; no life lost and no hint in Twirl; no helpers, hints or extra moves in Pounce, Bubbles and Toy Box; no hint, Undo or Restart in Catwalk, Patches, Sunbeams and Yarn; no hint, Undo or Restart in Open the Door! and Fair Play; no hint, Undo or Restart in Tunnel Run; no more misses than pairs, with no hint or Restart, in Copycat; the fewest taps, with no hint, in Night Light; no hint, Undo or Restart in Unravel, If It Fits and High Perch; no hint in Fuzzy Spelling; finishing within the pots, with no hint, Undo or Restart, in Cat Mat; no hint, Undo or Restart in Laser Pointer and Treat Time; par, with no hint, Undo or Restart, in Up a Tree; clearing a level on the first try in Tail Chase, and on the first try with no Shake or De-fluff in Wind Up; reaching Genius Cat (hints allowed) in Keyboard Cat; beating your best time, with no hint, in Tangle; the fewest moves in Knock It Off; an exact hit, with no hint, in Toe Beans; no hint (and, with Auto grade on, not a life lost) in Toydoku, where Undo, Undo guess and Restart are free; no hint, Undo, Shuffle or Restart in Pick of the Pile; no hint, Undo or Restart in Scratch That; no hint, Audit, Undo or Restart in Fridge Magnets and Purrfect Square; every place found on the first try, with no hint or Restart, in Catlas; all five cats in a round, each on the first tap, with no hint, in Cat Burglar; no hint, Undo, Restart or turn in Cat Pile; the fewest swaps, with no hint or Undo, in Fish Bones; finding the stash by the gold line (a few guesses more than a careful player needs), with no hint or Restart, in Toy Stash; guessing the hidden word before the last word is found, with no hint or Restart, in Knit Pick; every bird spotted, with not a tap wasted and no wiping, in Bird Watch; no hint in Shelf Life; no hint, Undo or Restart in Meowbile; no hint or Restart in Kitten Caboodle. Under the paw, a line says what earned it ("No undos!", "No hints used!"). Gold paws are counted on the device (`G.goldPaws`, counted once per win by the win card), and once a game has one, its front-page tile shows a little gold paw with the number on its pad, in the corner (on a Favorites chip, on the picture's corner). A gold paw on a game's top level or size masters it (Catlas needs one on both Hard · Earth and Disputed land, answered by List or Type, not Pick; in Toe Beans, level 10 in either mode): from then on its tile is gold, on the front page and in the All games menu, keeping its shelf's colour along its edge. Today's challenge in the menu goes gold too once it's solved, as on the front page.
- **Hints** show, they never play for you: a hint marks the square, piece or line to look at (gold for where to go, red for a mistake) and says what to do there, and you make the move. The mark stays on screen for five seconds (or until the board changes) rather than only flashing, so it shows even with animations turned off (the device's reduce-motion setting). Asking again on the same board says more: the first hint shows where to look, and the next names what goes there. In Catdoku, a row, column or colour, then the square; in Toydoku, High Perch, Open the Door!, Fair Play, Patches, Fridge Magnets and Purrfect Square, the square and why it's settled, then what goes in it; in Scratch That, the square, then what and why; in Sunbeams, the cat, then its whole sunbeam; in Catwalk, a perch, then the catwalk; in Treat Time, a row or column, then the pile; in Up a Tree, the letter, then what it becomes; in Toe Beans, the two numbers, then the sign; in Pick of the Pile, one block, then its pair; in Cat Mat, the pot, then the squares it soaks into; in If It Fits and Cat Pile, the piece, then its place; in Fish Bones, a bone and the letter its place wants, then the bone to swap it with; in Toy Stash, a box the paws have settled, then its toy; in Knit Pick, the square a word starts in, then the whole word; in Shelf Life, the cubby, then the ball that goes there (or for an empty cubby, the two balls to spin it from); in Meowbile, the kind of toy to work out next and the rods that give it away, then its weight; in Kitten Caboodle each hint shows one letter of the word, in its square, for you to type; and in Cat Burglar each ring is smaller. Every game's hints work through one helper in `games.js` (`G.hinter`). Every puzzle game has a Hint button (Catdoku has Audit too), Toy Box, Bubbles and Bird Watch have helpers, and Tail Chase, Pawlette and Bird Watch have no hints. Undo also works with Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) in every game that has it.
- **Help:** any help (a hint, Audit, a helper, extra moves, a revive) keeps you on the same level or size and costs the gold paw; your time still counts. A clean win moves you up. In Toe Beans, finishing with Close enough (within 10 but not exact) keeps you on the same level. In Cat Mat, running over the pots also keeps you on the same level (and in Fish Bones, running over the swaps), and in Pick of the Pile a shuffle does too. In Toy Stash only a gold paw moves you up: finding the stash past the gold line keeps you on the same level. In Catlas, a clean trip moves you on to the next round only with 8 found on the first try (or 7 and a bonus), or all 10 found. In Cat Burglar, a round moves you up with 4 or 5 cats caught and no hint.
- **Keys:** every game can be played from a keyboard. Where a game had no keys of its own, the arrow keys move a cursor (a blue outline, shown from the first arrow press; a tap or Escape hides it) and Space or Enter does what a tap does there, through one helper in `games.js` (`G.gridKeys`): boxes in Hide & Seek (M doodles a cat) and Copycat, cats in Night Light, squares in Catdoku (each also reached with Tab), patches in Patches (1 to 4 pick a fabric and sew it, Backspace unpicks), corners in Sunbeams (Space at one corner, then at the other; Backspace takes a sunbeam away), the strand in Yarn (the arrows lead it along from its end), perches in Catwalk, pegs in Unravel, balls in Tangle (Space picks one up, the arrows move it, Shift for bigger steps), squares of the mat in Twirl (it scrolls along when zoomed), free blocks in Pick of the Pile, baskets in Toy Box (Shift+Space sends the other front toy), a crosshair in Cat Burglar (finer steps as you zoom in), squares of the blanket in Cat Pile (1 to 3 pick a clump of cats from the tray, shown at the cursor, and Space puts it down; R turns it, with Turning on) the numbers, toe beans and pad in Toe Beans, bones in Fish Bones (Space picks one up and puts it down on another to swap them), and cubbies in Shelf Life (Space picks a ball and swaps it with the next, or picks an empty cubby and the two balls to spin it from). In Wind Up the left and right arrows move Gus along the rim, Down, Space or Enter drops the yarn, P pauses, S shakes the basket and L de-fluffs. In Meowbile the arrows pick a kind of toy, 1 to 9 weigh it, Backspace takes its weight away and Escape lets go. In Kitten Caboodle the letter keys type the picked kitten's word, Backspace takes a letter out, and Enter, Tab or the down arrow move on to the next kitten still asleep (the up arrow or Shift+Tab back). Toy Stash has keys of its own: 1 to 8 put a toy in, Shift with a number marks a toy as not in the stash, the arrows pick a box, Backspace takes a toy out and Enter checks. In Knit Pick the arrows move a cursor over the blanket and Space picks a word's first letter, then its last. In Bird Watch the arrows move a cursor over the panes and Space taps one, or 1 to 9 tap a pane straight away, laid out like a keypad (7 8 9 along the top), and P pauses. Escape lets go of the square picked in High Perch and Open the Door! as in Toydoku, and Ctrl+Shift+Z is left alone everywhere.
- **Guess:** in the logic games you fill in square by square (Catdoku, Toydoku, Fair Play, High Perch, Open the Door!, Scratch That, Purrfect Square and Fridge Magnets), Guess makes the next thing you put in a guess: the board is ringed in violet until you do, and the square then glows violet. Undo guess (shown while there's a guess) takes everything back to just before the latest guess in one tap, through the game's own Undo, so it costs what Undo costs there; guesses stack, and each Undo guess peels one off. Plain Undo past a guess, or Restart, ends it. One helper in `games.js` runs it (`G.guesser`). Sunbeams, Patches and Treat Time don't have it.
- **Win streaks:** every game counts the puzzles you win in a row on this device (`G.streak`), and from two on the win card says so ("🔥 3 wins in a row"). Leaving a puzzle you've started for a new one ends the streak, and so does losing: running out of lives, moves or track space, giving up (Give up, or Close enough in Toe Beans), getting tangled in Tail Chase, a Cat Burglar round with a cat missed, or waking the human or letting too many birds get away in Bird Watch. Catdoku also shows its streak and best under the title.
- **A phone held sideways:** the board takes the left of the screen at the screen's full height and stays put, and the title, status line, keys, buttons and settings scroll in a column beside it (`.stage` in `games.css`; `G.sideways()` tells a page's script when it's on). A board taller than it is wide is narrowed so all of it fits (`--aspect`). Keyboard Cat fits on one screen, the word typed, the keys and its few buttons on the left and the word list on the right; Meowbile lays out its mobile the same way with a layout of its own. Bubbles' board lies on its side there, the cat shooting left to right. Hide & Seek's floor lies sideways there when its boxes come out bigger that way (as in full screen), and a floor too big for the screen scrolls in its frame. Patches' cats and Yarn's tangled kitten, which hang outside the board, are put away there. Purrfect Square stands its numbers beside the square; Fridge Magnets makes the fridge as big as the screen's height allows, with the letters small in a slim strip of three beside it (and Crunch put away from beside the title); Toe Beans goes in three columns: the paw, the numbers beside it in two columns of three, then everything else; Toy Stash puts its tray of toys at the top of the column. A board much wider than tall keeps the whole width instead (`.stage.wide`): the title and menu share a slim row above it, so Fish Bones' fish fits under them, and Up a Tree puts away the tree itself there, so the words come out about twice as big: each word sits on a thick line in its perch's carpet colour, with Dottie beside the highest and the feather by the goal, and the keyboard and buttons beside them. Full screen keeps its own sideways layout. Held upright and on a laptop, the pages are as before.
- **Win cards on a phone held sideways:** the picture sits on the left and the words and buttons beside it, two buttons to a row, so the card fits on the screen rather than running off the bottom (Cat Burglar's card after each painting does the same).
- **Share:** every win card can share a result (game, level, time, gold paw) with a link to the game. Catdoku, Yarn, Patches, Sunbeams, Toe Beans, Knock It Off, Hide & Seek, Catwalk, Tangle, Fair Play, Open the Door!, Tunnel Run, Copycat, Night Light, Unravel, Keyboard Cat, If It Fits, High Perch, Fuzzy Spelling, Cat Mat, Laser Pointer, Treat Time, Up a Tree, Toydoku, Pick of the Pile, Scratch That, Fridge Magnets, Purrfect Square, Catlas, Cat Burglar, Cat Pile, Fish Bones, Toy Stash, Knit Pick, Shelf Life, Meowbile and Kitten Caboodle can also share a link to the exact puzzle, fresh or with your progress; Toy Box, Pounce, Bubbles, Twirl, Tail Chase, Bird Watch and Wind Up share the exact level, board, mat, rug, flock or drop order as dealt. Pawlette's Share sends your Swipe lines (or, in Zen, an invitation) with a link to the game. A link that doesn't work (cut off, or changed by hand) says so and leaves the puzzle that was out, or, when the page had none, starts a new one; every game checks a link's puzzle before opening it (`G.offerShare` in `games.js`).
- **Toys:** the Toys button opens the toy chest, one choice for every game that uses toys. A game that needs toys in more colours than are picked opens it and says how many it needs.

## Features

- Board sizes from 5 × 5 to 14 × 14, each with a unique solution
- Puzzles generated in a background Web Worker, with the next one prefetched
- Auto grade and auto exclude toggles
- Paw marks (🐾) by tapping or dragging, with an erase mode
- Hint: first a mistake in red (a cat where none goes, or your own paw where a cat goes); otherwise the row, column or colour with the fewest spots left for its cat glows gold, and asking again shows the square
- Hint, undo, restart, a free audit, and lives with revives; Restart clears the board but keeps any lives lost, unless they've run out
- Wins move you up a size; a win that needed a hint, Audit or a revive, or running out of lives, keeps you at the same size; a win without losing a life, a hint or Audit earns a gold paw, with Higgins stretched out on the win card in place of the cat photo
- Higgins, a brown tabby, is Catdoku's mascot: every cat you place on the board is Higgins's face (and so is every cat in the counter, the How to play walkthrough and the cat facts), Higgins sits beside the title, and Higgins is the tabby on the front-page icon
- Progress saved in `localStorage`
- Cat photos and breeds from public cat APIs (all optional; the game works offline), and its 🔥 win streak and best under the title

## Yarn

A second puzzle in `yarn/`, linked from the top of every page and sharing Catdoku's look and home-screen app.
Lead one strand through balls of yarn from lightest to darkest, passing through every square once.
The skein, the order of the colours from Start to End with its colourway's name, sits under the board, as every game's key does, with a kitten pouncing on its Start and another tangled in its End.
Every puzzle is generated in the browser and checked to have exactly one solution.
Hint marks the next square for the strand, or in red the last square it was right on if it has gone wrong.
Shared styling and helpers live in `games.css` and `games.js`.

Every game has a timer that starts on your first move and pauses while the page is in the background or a card is open. Tap it to hide the time.
Your fastest times for each game and size are kept on your device and shown under Best times.
Share sends a link to the exact puzzle, either fresh or with your moves so far (to ask for help). The puzzle travels inside the link, so no server is involved.
Every game moves you up as you win: the next puzzle is a size bigger (or, in Toy Box, the next level). If you needed help (in Catdoku, Audit or running out of lives; in Toy Box, a helper or a jam), you stay at the same size or level. The finishing card, and Catdoku's out-of-lives and Toy Box's jam cards, always let you pick a different size or level instead.

## Patches

A third puzzle in `patches/`: sew a quilt by giving every patch one of four fabrics so no two patches that share a seam match.
The fabrics sit under the quilt, as every game's pad sits under its board, with a calico stretching out beneath them and a tabby napping on the quilt's top corner. Patches with a button come already sewn. Not sure of a patch? Pick Marker (after the four fabrics) and tap it to put your own button on it as a reminder; it doesn't sew the patch, and tapping again takes it off. Every quilt is generated in the browser and checked to have exactly one solution.
Hint names the fabric for the open patch with the fewest choices left, or tints a patch in the wrong fabric red.

## Sunbeams

A fourth puzzle in `sunbeams/`, a cat-themed Shikaku: every number is a cat, and each cat needs a rectangle of sunlight with exactly that many squares, until the whole floor is sunny.
Hint lights the squares of a sunbeam still to place (the one with the fewest ways to fit), or a wrong sunbeam in red.
Each sunbeam gets a sleeping cat of the right size and shape, stretched out to fill it. The cat pictures live in `cats/` so any game can use them.

## Toy Box

A fifth game in `toybox/`, a cat-toy sorting game, starring Laya, a grey cat with brown eyes: she sits beside the title, lies on the rug inside the ball track watching the toys go round (fading away whenever a basket needs the space inside the loop, and back once it's gone), and struts off on the win card for a gold paw (her face is on the front-page icon too). Tap a wicker basket to tip its front toys onto a winding ball track; each toy drops into the first basket it reaches whose front toy matches, or the first empty basket, so timing a tap decides where toys go. Toys go once round before they can return to their own basket. If the two front toys differ, the one you tap is the one sent, and a toy can only leave once the whole row in front of it has gone. A toy alone in its row sits in the middle, so it reads as the front of the basket. Short tips pop up the first time two different front toys appear and the first time the track is nearly full. Fill a basket with eight of one toy and it's carried off, basket and all, so there are fewer baskets to work with as you go.
There are 21 levels, picked from a list at the top; they get harder up to level 21: up to nine of the ten kinds of toy, a track that shrinks from 16 spaces to 5, more hidden toys and more split pairs. The track holds fewer toys as levels go on, and more toys start hidden in the back rows of baskets (a level hides none, or at least four toys of at least two kinds). From level 4 some matching pairs are dealt split up, so toys aren't always side by side with their match. If the track fills and nothing can find a basket, it jams. Every level is checked by a solver in a background Web Worker before it's dealt, so it can always be won with the right timing (the solver follows the same first-basket rule, and treats which rolling toy reaches an empty basket first as the player's choice). Toys are pictures from the shared toy library in `toys/`: ten kinds, one per palette colour, and each level picks one picture per colour (drawn stand-ins are used if a picture is missing). Each level has one of each helper: Shake a basket (rearranges one basket you pick), Shuffle all baskets (picks the mix that gives the most rolling toys somewhere to go), Lose a color (takes away every toy of one color) and Add a small basket (holds two toys). When the track jams, any helpers left are offered. Toys lets players leave out any toy pictures they don't like (kept on their device). Levels use up to nine colours, so at most one whole colour can be left out; a dealt level swaps it for a colour it doesn't use, which the solver doesn't mind since kinds are only labels to it. There's a timer with best times for each level, and a cat photo when you finish a level. New baskets deals another level of the same number, Restart sets out these baskets again, and Share sends a link to these exact baskets.

## Pounce

A sixth game in `pounce/`, a match-3 game with the toys from the toy library, starring Crunch, a grey tabby who sits beside the title, creeps up from the corner of the mat to cheer whenever one swap sets off a chain of matches, and rolls about on the win card for a gold paw (Crunch is also on the front-page icon). Swap two toys next to each other (drag one onto the other, or tap one then the other) to line up three or more of a kind, and catch the toys each level asks for before the moves run out. Toys above drop into the gaps and new ones fall in, so one swap can set off more lines. Four in a line makes a toy with the zoomies that clears its row or column when it's matched, an L or T makes a bouncy toy that clears the squares around it, and five makes catnip, which clears every toy of the kind it's swapped with. Swapping two special toys sets both off. If no swap is left, the toys are shaken up.
There are 20 levels with more kinds of toy, more kinds to catch and more of each as you go; the move limits were tuned with a simulated player so it wins nearly every time at level 1 and about half the time at level 20. Running out of moves offers five more moves once per level (and then the level comes round again) or a fresh try. There's a timer with best times for each level, and a cat photo when you finish a level. Arrow keys and Space work too. New board deals another board at the same level, Restart puts this board back as it was dealt, and Share sends a link to it (zen has New board only). At the end of the level list, Zen has no goals, no move limit and no timer, and just counts the toys you catch; it keeps your place in the levels for when you go back. Toys lets players leave out any toy pictures they don't like (kept on their device); a colour with no pictures left isn't dealt, and at least six colours have to stay in play since the bigger levels and zen use six kinds.

## Twirl

A seventh game in `twirl/`, starring Bugsy, a fluffy brown and white cat who wants to nap on the mat: Bugsy curls up beside the title, peeks over the top corner of the mat while you play, and dozes in a box on the win card for a gold paw (Bugsy is also on the front-page icon). Satin ribbons cover the whole mat; tap one and it slides off the way its pointed end points, following its own path, but only if nothing is in the way. Hint lights a ribbon that's free to go. A blocked tap costs a life, as in Catdoku: three lives, and running out offers a restart, new ribbons, or three more lives (up to nine). There are 20 levels, plus a level 0 with a 9 × 14 mat where the ribbons show twice as big as on level 1; the levels run from an 18 × 27 mat of shorter ribbons to a 28 × 42 mat of long, winding ones; a level finished on the first three lives moves you up. Big mats zoom (pinch, − and +, or Ctrl/⌘ + scroll) and you drag to look around. The button beside − Fit + (or F) goes full screen: the mat takes all the room the screen has, laid sideways when its squares come out bigger that way (on a laptop, 28 × 42 squares go from about 13 pixels to 21), with the lives above and Hint, Restart and zoom below (on a phone held sideways, in a column beside it). Taps, the arrow keys and the walkthrough follow the mat round. New ribbons lays out another mat at the same level, and Share sends a link to this mat (colours are picked afresh for whoever opens it).
Every ribbon wears its own colourway from `colorways.js` (shared with Yarn), chosen to stand apart from the ribbons it touches. Early levels have solid ribbons in a mix of flat and twisty textures, which makes them easy to tell apart; later levels shade each ribbon through its colourway (ombré), and from level 13 each board is all twisty or all flat (picked at random), so you follow ribbons by shape. A Twisty ribbons toggle (kept on the device) draws every ribbon flat on every level for anyone who'd rather. Ribbons come as coils, spirals, staircases, zigzags and winding paths.
Mats are made by covering every square with ribbons, then pointing each one and playing the board through: when nothing can move, a ribbon is turned round (or, as a last resort, cut in two) so something can, choosing the one that opens up latest so few moves are open at once. The playthrough is a solution, and the board is played through again to be sure, so every mat can be cleared.

## Toe Beans

An eighth game in `toebeans/`, a numbers puzzle on a cat's paw. Six numbers sit in tiles above the paw; the paw's four toe beans are + − × ÷ and its big pad shows the goal. Tap a number, a toe bean, then another number: the two combine and the answer takes the second number's place. Make the goal exactly (you don't have to use every number) and every pad on the paw fills at once in gold: a golden paw.
There are 10 levels; higher ones have bigger goals that need more steps (two at level 1, five at level 10). Hitting the goal moves you up a level. Once your closest number is within 10 of the goal, Close enough (greyed out until then) ends the game there and keeps your level; the card says how close ("So close: 3 away!") and shows one way it could have been made. Undo is free. Hint lights the two numbers and the toe bean for the first step of a shortest way to the goal, and costs the gold paw. Every goal is checked in a background Web Worker to need exactly the level's number of steps. There's a timer with best times for each level, a share link, and a cat photo when you hit the goal.

Reverse, a switch under the level picker, turns it round: the big pad starts on a number and you bring it down to 0. The pad can be used in the sums: tap the pad, a toe bean, then a number (or a number, a toe bean, then the pad) and the answer goes on the pad, using up the number; numbers can still combine with each other too, and 0 comes from taking away the pad's own number. Every step uses up one number, so a way to 0 takes as many steps as numbers it uses; puzzles are made by working out, for every set of the numbers, which start numbers it can bring to 0, and the start is picked to need exactly the level's number of steps. Blankets are made in the page (a few milliseconds each): the level's words are placed at random, each crossing letters already down where it can, until the squares left over are few enough for a hidden word of exactly that length, which fills them in reading order; every listed word must then be in the blanket exactly once, either way round, and none inside another.

There are 6 levels: 6 × 6 with words across and down, 7 × 7 adding diagonally down, 8 × 8 adding diagonally up, 9 × 9 adding backwards, then 10 × 10 and 11 × 11 every way, with about 6 to 25 words and more letters left over (up to 18) the bigger the blanket. On a phone held sideways the word list sits beside the blanket.

Hint lights the square a word still to find starts in, and asked again, the whole word. A hint keeps you on the same level; guessing the hidden word before the last word is found, with no hint or Restart, earns a gold paw. Restart hides every word again on the same blanket. There's a walkthrough on the first visit, a timer with best times for each level, and a share link (the blanket, its pattern and its words, with the words found so far if you like).

## Bird Watch

A forty-first game in `birdwatch/`, a cat-themed whack-a-mole, on the Kitten Play shelf, starring Bugsy and Bowtie (`bowtie-3` stands up on his back legs beside the title, and the two of them are up at the window together on the gold-paw card): three tall double-hung windows look out on a tree, a lawn and a pond, each window three panes high, and birds land outside the panes for a moment. Tap a pane while its bird is there to spot it: a pink paw print presses on the glass and the bird flies off. The birds are a robin, a bluebird, a goldfinch, a cardinal and a blue tit, drawn in the page from the site's palette; from level 3 falling leaves drift past too, and from level 5 squirrels scamper by, and those are to be left alone.

Each level sends a fixed flock, from 12 birds on level 1 to 34 on level 12, each level a little quicker (a bird stays from about 1.7 seconds down to 0.6, and up to three things can be at the windows at once). Up to a quarter of the flock may fly off unspotted; one more and the level's lost ("Too many got away!"). The human is napping nearby (Zzz drift up from the bottom of the room): every tap on the glass makes a little noise, bird or no bird, and a level allows the flock plus three taps, so the tap after that wakes them up and the level's lost ("The human woke up!"). A tap on empty glass or a decoy flashes the pane red and wastes a tap; in the last three, the count turns red and the Zzz go faint. Every bird spotted leaves a smudgy paw print on its pane, and the prints build up until the birds behind them are hard to see; the helper above the buttons, Wipe the windows, cleans them all off, twice a level. Seeing the flock through moves you up a level; a wipe keeps you on the same level, as Toy Box's helpers do, and spotting every bird with not a tap wasted and no wiping earns a gold paw.

The flock is worked out in advance from a seed (which windows, when, which birds and decoys, how long each stays), so Restart brings the same flock again, New flock brings another, and Share sends a link to the flock. Pause (or P) stops the run, and so does leaving the page. There's no clock, since a run lasts as long as its flock: Best scores shows the most birds spotted on each level and how many times it's been won. The arrow keys and Space tap a pane, or the number keys 1 to 9 tap one straight away, laid out like a keypad (7 8 9 along the top). There's a walkthrough on the first visit, which ends by showing the numbers on the panes.

## Shelf Life

A forty-second game in `shelflife/`, a cat-themed colour-gradient sort (the I Love Hue kind of puzzle), on the Curious Cat shelf, starring Fred (`fred-9` sits beside the title, `fred-23` prowls on the floor below the shelf, Dottie (`dottie-22`) settles in there with her crochet once it's sorted, and `fred-16` crouches to pounce on the gold-paw card): a pale painted shelf of cubbies in a yarn shop, each holding a shop-wound skein of yarn: nine drawings of skeins (`shelflife/skeins/`, all near enough square, so their shapes don't tell balls apart), each kept as its strands in ink and its outline, which is filled with the ball's colour under the strands, so every skein shades its colour as the drawing does. Each ball keeps one skein shape on a shelf, wherever it's swapped to. The balls shade smoothly from corner to corner, and Fred has knocked them out of order. Tap one ball, then another, to swap them, or drag one onto another. On a phone, a swipe that starts anywhere but on a loose ball (the floor, the frame, a tagged ball) scrolls the page. Balls with a white shop tag on them stay put. Sort every ball into its place and the shelf is done. A Shelf switch (kept on the device) takes the shelf away, leaving just the yarn, bigger, the skeins all but touching.

Each shelf picks four corner colours from the site's palette, all from one side of the colour wheel so the blends between them stay colourful, with one corner paled and the one across from it darkened; every cubby's colour is blended between the corners in OKLab. Shelves are kept only when no two balls are closer in colour than the level allows, and when the middle isn't grey. Every loose ball starts out of its place. There are 12 levels: 1 to 9 grow from a 4 × 5 shelf with every edge ball tagged, through tagged sides and tagged pins (the corners, the middles of the edges and the centre), to 8 × 10 with only the corners tagged and close colours. On levels 10 to 12 Fred has unwound a few balls too, leaving empty cubbies (dashed rings): tap one, then two balls, to spin a new ball that's their blend. Two balls the same distance either side of it in its row or column give exactly its colour; a wrong blend can be spun again. The status line counts swaps beside the fewest the shelf could take.

Hint marks a cubby to fix, choosing the one with the most settled neighbours, and asked again rings the ball that goes there; once every ball is sorted, it marks an empty cubby, then the two balls to spin it from. A spun ball in the wrong shade shows in red. It never moves a ball for you. A hint keeps you on the same level, and a solve with no hint earns a gold paw (Undo and Restart are free, since undoing a swap is only swapping back). There's a walkthrough on the first visit, a timer with best times for each level, keys (the arrows move a cursor over the cubbies, and Space picks a ball, swaps, or picks an empty cubby and the balls to spin it from), and a share link to the shelf, fresh or with your swaps. Full screen was left out: the board is a tall shelf that fits an upright phone, at about 35 pixels a cubby on an iPhone SE at the biggest level.

## Meowbile

A forty-third game in `meowbile/`, a cat-themed balance puzzle like SolveMe Mobiles: toys hang from a mobile in strings, and the mobile hangs perfectly level, so both ends of every rod weigh the same. Each kind of toy weighs a whole number from 1 to 9, and the puzzle is to work out what each kind weighs. One clue starts it off: a tag at the hook with what the whole mobile weighs, or one kind's weight. Tap a toy (on the mobile, or its button in the row under it) and then a number; a rod whose toys are all weighed tips towards its heavier end, so a wrong weight shows. The status line keeps a running total of what the toys weighed so far come to ("So far: 26 of 40", out of the tag's weight when that's the clue), then what the whole mobile weighs once every toy is weighed. The puzzle is solved when every weight is right.

Every mobile is made in the browser: a random shape of rods (at most three deep), a weight for each kind, and a whole weight that each string of toys must weigh its share of, then a string of toys that weighs that much for each end. It's kept only if every kind is used, no rod has the same toys at both ends, and a step-by-step solver can work every weight out from the clue, so there's exactly one answer. There are 11 levels, from 2 kinds of toy on one rod to 6 kinds on six rods; levels 6 and 8 to 10 need at least one step that puts two rods together (two equations in two unknowns), and 1 to 5 and 7 never do. Levels 1, 2 and 4 always have the tag, 3, 5 and 10 always a toy's weight, and 6 to 9 either. Level 11 (5 kinds on four rods) gives nothing away: the tag just says Lightest, and the answer is the lightest whole weights that balance every rod (doubling them all would balance too). Its mobiles are kept only when the rods alone fix how every kind compares with the others and the weights have no common factor, so the lightest answer is the only one; the first one gets a short walkthrough of its own, and its first hint starts from the lightest kind of toy. The toys come from the toy library, one from each colour, and the Toys button chooses which.

Hint marks a weight that's wrong in red; otherwise it rings the kind of toy to work out next and marks the rods (or the tag) that give it away, and asked again says its weight. It never puts a weight in for you. A hint keeps you on the same level, and a solve with no hint, Undo or Restart earns a gold paw, with a general cat (`c18-38`) leaping on the card; another general cat (`c18-21`) looks up from beside the title. There's a walkthrough on the first visit, a timer with best times for each level, keys (the arrows pick a kind of toy and 1 to 9 weigh it), and a share link to the mobile, fresh or with your weights. On a phone held sideways, the mobile takes the left of the screen at its full height and stays in view, with everything else in a column beside it (toys about 33 pixels across on the biggest mobiles on an iPhone SE, rather than 21). Full screen was left out: the board fits a phone held upright, with toys about 34 pixels across on the biggest mobiles, and every toy can also be picked from the row of buttons under the mobile.

## Kitten Caboodle

A forty-fourth game in `kittencaboodle/`, a cat-themed Rootonym, on the Cat Spells shelf: a mother cat sits at the top with a word root on her sign (UN-, TELE-, CHRONO-, -LESS…), what it means, and where it comes from ("from Ancient Greek τῆλε (têle)"). Under her, a row for each of her kittens, asleep and grey: each kitten is an everyday word made from the root, with a square for each letter. Tap a kitten (or press Next) and its clue shows under the board, with the language the word came into English from when there is one ("from Middle English", a sign that an old word's meaning may have moved on from its root's: UPSET was "to set up"). Type the word and the kitten wakes up in colour; a wrong word shakes its row (Backspace, or tap a square to take letters out from there). Wake every kitten to win. The words spelled so far are listed under the game, each linked to Wiktionary with where it comes from.

The kittens are kitten Gus, Peanut, Marmalade and Willow (`kitten-…`, `marmalade-kitten-…`, `willow-kitten-…`), a different picture for each word, and the mother is one of 24 general cats sitting up (Purrfect Square's sitters); kitten Gus asleep on kitten Peanut (`kitten-7`) is on the gold-paw card. The kittens are as big as the screen's height allows with the clue and keys still showing, so a litter of four gets bigger kittens than a litter of seven.

The roots and words are in `words/roots.json`, made by `tools/make-roots.py` from kaikki.org's copy of Wiktionary (Wiktionary's own site refuses cloud sessions) and SCOWL: 116 roots picked by hand from those Wiktionary gives, each with up to 20 everyday words built on it, the word's Wiktionary definition as its clue (an everyday meaning before a technical one, and never one that gives the word away) and its etymology. A word is a root's kitten when Wiktionary builds it from the root in English, or it comes from the root's own Greek or Latin word with the root in its spelling; words that only look built on a root (UNTIL, MALE, and OUTRAGE, which Wiktionary says isn't out- + rage) are left out, as are words about killing, illness or sex. A few roots' meanings are written plainly where Wiktionary's are about grammar ("Used to form adjectives from nouns, with the sense of being full of" is "full of"). There are 8 levels: 4 to 7 kittens, everyday words first, and the root's letters filled into the squares on levels 1 to 4, only on the mother's sign on 5 and 6, and hidden until the first word is spelled on 7 and 8.

Hint shows one letter in its square: the right letter in red where one typed is wrong, otherwise the next letter in gold; you type it in. A hint keeps you on the same level, and a litter woken with no hint or Restart earns a gold paw. There's a walkthrough on the first visit, a timer with best times for each level, the keyboard (on screen and real), and a share link to the litter, fresh or with your letters. On a phone held sideways, the den takes the left of the screen at its full height, with the clue and keys beside it. Full screen was left out: typing goes with play, and the on-screen keys take half the screen anyway.

## Wind Up

A forty-fifth game in `windup/`, a cat-themed Suika, on the Kitten Play shelf: Gus sits on the rim of a wicker basket and drops balls of yarn into it, and two balls of the same size that touch wind into one of the next size up, ten sizes in all. Point where Gus should drop (or drag), let go to drop; the arrow keys move him and Down, Space or Enter drops (P pauses, S shakes, L de-fluffs). Peanut watches from the corner beside the next ball. A level is won by winding its goal ball; the yarn piling up over the dashed line for about two and a half seconds loses it, though the card that says so offers Shake (and De-fluff, if it's still there and there's fluff to take) to keep going, at the cost of the gold paw. The try only counts, and a win streak only ends, once you start again or leave it.

There's no physics library: the balls are circles with gravity, no bounce between them, a little friction, a bounce off the floor (yarn a little, fluffballs more), spin from rolling and rubbing that dies away in under a second, and shapes in the basket they roll off, all written for the page (`step()` in `windup/index.html`; impulses stop balls moving into each other, then overlaps are eased apart by moving them without giving them speed, so a pile settles quietly). The ten sizes of yarn are drawn by `yarnBall` in `colorways.js`, in colours that jump about so neighbouring sizes differ in colour and lightness; a Numbers switch writes each size on its ball. Two balls winding together swing round each other as the bigger one comes up. Fluffballs, Fuzzy Spelling's grey pompom with googly eyes (`fuzzyspelling/pompom.webp` and `eye.webp`), come now and then (not often) among the drops from level 4: they wind only with other fluffballs, up to size 6 (two of those puff away), and never count toward the goal.

Ten levels, each a little harder: a goal ball from size 6 up to 10, bigger balls among the drops, a lower line (less room), more fluffballs, and from level 2 a basket with a sloped floor, a hump, ledges off the walls, or round corners. The drops come in a fixed order for each puzzle (a seed), so Restart deals the same basket, and the Share link opens the same level and drop order; New basket deals another. A timer with best times, a pause, and a gold paw for winning a level on the first try with no helpers (no hint or Undo here: it's played in real time). Shake is the helper for a stuck pile: the basket jolts and tilts and every ball is thrown up and sideways, sometimes most of the way up the basket, and the balls get a few seconds before they count as over the line again; it waits a few seconds between shakes, and costs the gold paw. De-fluff is a lint roller that takes every fluffball off the board, once a board (it only shows on levels that have fluffballs, doesn't use up the go if none are there, and costs the gold paw too). When the goal ball is wound it pops up and rings of colour spread out from it for a moment before the win card. A walkthrough plays on the first visit, there's a full screen mode, and the board is laid out beside the buttons on a phone held sideways. The win card has Gus and Peanut as kittens (`kitten-1`).

## Toy library

`toys/` holds 32 cat toy pictures for any game to use: 256 × 256 WebP with a clear background, named by what they are (`wiffle-red`, `fish`, `cork-paw`…). The ones in `toys/` are recoloured to the games' palette; `toys/original/` has the same toys in their first colours, and `toys/earlier/` an earlier set.
