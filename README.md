# Lap Cat Puzzles

A family of cat-themed puzzle games, each a single HTML page, at [lapcatpuzzles.com](https://lapcatpuzzles.com). The
site was called Catdoku, after its first game, until November 2026, and this repository still is; saved data keeps its
`catdoku.` keys. The first game, Catdoku (in `catdoku/`), is a logic puzzle: place one 🐱 in every row, every column, and every color region — and no two cats may touch, not even diagonally.

## Hosting and the move

The site is served by Cloudflare Workers (static assets, no Worker code) from this repository's `main` branch: on
every push Cloudflare runs `sh tools/build-site.sh`, which puts everything but the notes, `tools/` and `.claude/` in
`_site`, then `npx wrangler deploy`, which serves `_site` as `wrangler.jsonc` says (`_headers` sets its headers). The
domain, lapcatpuzzles.com, and its email forwarding are in the same Cloudflare account.

It used to be GitHub Pages, at `rhymes-with-tree.github.io/catdoku/`. A browser keeps saved data apart for each
address, so `move.js`, loaded first by every page, carries players' progress across. At the old address, once the new
site answers (it reads the new `version.txt`, which `_headers` lets any page read), a page goes on to the same page at
the new one with everything saved packed into the link after `#move=` (which never reaches a server), and the new site
unpacks it before anything else runs. Until then the old site carries on as it was. An iPhone or iPad Home Screen app
keeps its own saved data, so it goes to `moved.html` instead: a note with a progress code to paste into Settings (Bring
over your progress). Android's Home Screen apps share the browser's, so they go straight on. GoatCounter is told each
page's path with `/catdoku` in front, as before, so the numbers carry on. The What's new card (news v3) tells movers
about the new name, at the new address only.

## Play

Open `index.html` in any modern browser: the front page shows the shelves of games side by side, one at a time (the owner's call, 2026-11-12, when everything in one list had grown very long). Today's challenge is at the top, above the search box. Under the search box the shelves' names are tabs, and the shelves stand in a row: a swipe goes exactly one shelf along, never stopping between two (the page moves the row itself; the browser's own snapping flew most of the way along on an iPhone), and the next shelf's edge shows at the side of the screen, and on a first visit the row nudges across and back once so it's plain that it moves. Each shelf starts with a title card right across it (its name, its line and how many games). Favorites is the first shelf once a game is starred, the shelf last looked at is remembered on the device (`catdoku.shelf.v1`), and searching stacks up every shelf that has a match. There are seven shelves: Cat Logic, Cat Math, Cat Trails, Cat Spells, Curious Cat, Kitten Play (match, pop and merge) and On the Prowl (chase, spot and pounce: the action and looking games, split off Kitten Play on 2026-11-12 under the owner's name for it: Tail Chase, Bird Watch, Pawlinko, Pounce Up, Cat Burglar and Catlas, with Pawstcards waiting in beta; Copycat is matching, so it stays on Kitten Play). Within a shelf, the front page has a tile for every game, with an icon drawn from its board, a line about it (on wider screens; a phone shows three small tiles across, just the picture and the name), how many you've solved on this device once you've played, and a New badge on the newest games (added in the last week, at most four). The tiles are in one grid, shelf by shelf, each shelf starting a new row with a label tile in its colour, a little apart from the one before, with every game's tile in its shelf's colour and the games A to Z on each shelf (in the All games menu too; `G.onShelf` in `games.js`); the shelves are by kind (Cat Logic for logic puzzles, Cat Math for numbers, Cat Trails for lines and paths, Cat Spells for words, Curious Cat for brain teasers and Kitten Play for quick games), after today's challenge (a wide tile at the start of the grid) and a Favorites label with the games you've starred on this device (every tile has a ☆, and on each game's page it's Add to favorites in the game's menu; the label only shows once you've starred one, and until then a line under the search box says how). A row of shortcuts under the search box jumps to each shelf, and the search box narrows them by name, line, shelf (by its name or a plain one, like "logic" or "words") or the puzzle a game is based on ("minesweeper" finds Hide & Seek). Every game's All games menu has the same search box, Favorites row and shelves (in the same shelf colours, from `G.SHELF_COLOUR` in `games.js`), with a star on every tile. Every win card, and every card that ends a game (out of lives, out of moves, no room and so on), has a Back to the front page button (`G.homeBtn`). The shelves, search words and favorites are kept in `games.js` (`G.GAMES`, `G.GROUPS`, `G.favorites`, `G.favStar`). No build step or dependencies. At the very bottom are Settings, Launch history and ☕ Buy me a coffee (the owner's page, buymeacoffee.com/lapcatpuzzles, opening in a new tab, 2026-11-18; see Buy me a coffee). (Catdoku used to be the front page; old share links to it are passed on to `catdoku/`.) The icons are in `icons/`, drawn by `tools/make-icons.py` (`python3 tools/make-icons.py` draws them again).

**Games in beta** are on the site but not on the front page yet: their pages work when opened by their address, and Launch history (`calendar/`) has an In beta line with a link to each, read from `BETA`, so a game leaves that line by itself the day it joins `GAMES` and shows on the calendar from then on (for
sharing with a few people while they're tuned), with a Beta label beside the title (a tap on it sends feedback), and they're kept out of the games
menu, search, favorites, the daily challenge and search engines (`BETA` in `games.js`, beside `GAMES`). Today:
Case Clawsed, at `stash/` (back in beta since 2026-11-24, the owner's call; see its section), Under the Blanket, at `blanket/` (back in beta since 2026-11-16; see its section), Swap-a-Pom, at `swapapom/` (swap letter pompoms in a craft tray to spell words, with Moonpie for company and her own purr; see its section), Tape Trap, at `tapetrap/` (lay a square of tape for every cat to sit in, starring Marmalade; see its section), Boing!, at `boing/` (stand springs on the floor and the furniture to bounce a toy ball into Ferguson's basket; see its section), and Pawstcards, at `pawstcards/` (Pinecone is on vacation and won't say where: ask what she's learned about the history, geography, politics, economy, people, culture or cats of where she is, and guess the country from her postcards, which are passages from Wikipedia with the place's name under her paw prints; see `IDEAS.md`, `TODO.md` and `tools/pawstcards/NOTES.md`).

## Across every game

- **Today's challenge:** one game a day is featured for everyone, at the top of the front page, at the top of every game's menu, and as a badge on that game's page. Winning any puzzle in it that day does the challenge, and the win card says so ("Today's challenge solved! 4-day streak!"), with the streak of days in a row kept on this device. Before then the front page asks "Can you solve one today?" (or "Solve one today for a 4-day streak"). The games come round in cycles from 2026-10-18: the first starts with Catdoku and then takes the rest in a shuffled order, each later cycle is shuffled afresh, and the same game never comes up two days running. A new game, given an added date in `G.GAMES`, joins the first cycle that starts at least 3 days after it was added, so it has a few days on the site first (see `daily` in `games.js`).
- **How to play:** the first visit plays a short walkthrough on the real board (everything else dims and a short line says what to look at), following Apple's onboarding guidance: brief, interactive and optional. Each step is one short line (six words or fewer), with "Tap to go on" only on the first, in cream on a dark card with a cream edge (since 2026-11-12, when the owner found the outlined letters laid straight over the board hard to read; "Let's Play!" too). A row of dots at the bottom shows how far through it is, and Skip (or Escape) ends it at any point; skipped counts as seen. On a first visit it keeps to the board and its rules: steps that only point at a tool or a setting (Erase, Bookmark, Hint…) are kept back as tips, and a switch (Auto grade, Auto check…) is taught only the first time it's turned on (`catdoku.tips.v1`), each shown once, later, by itself with a Got it button: the Hint tip once a started puzzle has gone 45 seconds without a move, the others a few seconds after the first move on the board in a visit, at most one a day. A tip for something you've already used is dropped unseen. A step can be interactive (`try` in `G.spotlight`): only its lit squares take taps, it waits for you to do the thing, says so in green and goes on; Every game's walkthrough has at least one such Your turn step, doing its main move on the real board (sewing a patch, drawing a sunbeam, tipping a mirror, making a sum, swapping two bones…) except the action games (Bubbles, Tail Chase, Bird Watch, Wind Up, L-M-N-O-Pom), where the first round is the practice, and Cat Burglar and Catlas, where the first move is the answer. Most put the board back afterwards (`G.yourTurn`); a first move that gives nothing away is kept: Hide & Seek's first box, a ribbon in Twirl, a clump in Cat Pile, a match in Pounce and a basket in Toy Box. Steps marked `later` (rarer rules, gold-paw details) wait for How to play. A step can also light a button to press (its taps go through too, and nowhere else). Catdoku has four in a row: tap the middle square twice for a cat ("🐾 means no cat here"), drag along three squares for paws, tap Erase, then drag the paws away; the board goes back as it was afterwards (and if you skip partway). Its Bookmark and Hint tips say what each is for ("Not sure? Bookmark, then try a 🐱. Return to Bookmark undoes it"), and Auto Notes and Auto grade say theirs the first time they're turned on. Space or Skip goes past one. The How to play button plays the whole walkthrough, tips included, at any point in a puzzle, and leaves your progress as it was: Copycat and Unravel then point at your own boxes or strand rather than opening boxes or drawing a way through, Tail Chase and Bird Watch pause the run first, and Pounce, Bubbles, Toy Box and Twirl wait for anything moving or loading to settle. There's no rules card. If the puzzle is finished, it says to start a new one first (New puzzle, or Restart where there's no New button). Mew Hue's walkthrough shows each rule by doing it: a step sets out a few blocks on an empty board, you make the slide (a mix, a line, stone, black and white), and it says what happened; your own board comes back afterwards. Each level does this for what's new on it the first time it comes up. The rules aren't printed on the page; Pawlette keeps its colour wheel and Catwalk its direction tiles.
- **What's new:** someone who's played before (anything saved on the device) gets a card once, the first time they open a game after a change worth telling them about (`whatsNew` in `games.js`). Since 2026-11-14 it's sound: every game has it, on unless it's turned off in Settings (or a game's own ☰ Menu), the other new options in Settings, and that an iPhone's silent switch keeps the games quiet, with Open Settings and Got it (kept in `catdoku.news.v2`). The first card, from the November 2026 redesign (`.v1`: ☰ Menu, free Undo and Bookmark, the red tools that cost the gold paw), came down then, as every tester had seen it. New visitors get the walkthrough instead and never see it.
- **Sound:** on unless it's turned off: on the Settings page (Sound, for every game at once, or a game at a time) or with Sound in a game's own ☰ Menu (kept in `catdoku.sound.v2`, `{slug: false}` for a game that's off; a beta game has only its menu's switch). A browser plays nothing until the first tap or key, so it starts then. Every tap after that wakes it again if the phone has put it to sleep, and the tap's own sound plays once it's awake rather than being lost; if it's still silent a moment later (an iPhone can leave it stuck after a call, Siri or another app's sound), it starts afresh (2026-11-16, from Twirl's feedback). Leaving a page lets its sound go (`pagehide`), and the first tap back makes it again, since an iPhone that brought a page back with Back (from the front page or Settings) left it silent; and if the sound says it's running but its clock hasn't moved a moment after a tap, it starts afresh too (2026-11-20, from Bed Hog's feedback). Every sound is made in the browser as it plays (Web Audio: oscillators, noise and filters, no files; `G.sound` in `games.js`), each the owner's pick from a listening page of candidates (2026-11-14, https://claude.ai/artifact/AiHXuVwvwB5CJSBPvgGNKS). Each note, thump or swoosh lasts 55% as long as when the owner picked it (`LENGTH`; they went on too long, the owner's call the same day), its notes still starting when they did; the shortest (0.06 s or less) stay as they are. The listening page has a Length slider to try others. The page tells Safari it's background sound, so it plays alongside someone's music and an iPhone's silent switch mutes it; it stops while the page is hidden. Every game gets the win card's (a music box; a marimba ta-da until the owner swapped it, the same day), the gold paw's (big bells), the lose card's (two marimba notes down, "oh well") and a hint's (a sparkle), all from the shared cards; a game can play its own instead (`sound: false` on the card) and add its own sounds (`G.sound.define`, from the pieces in `G.sound.kit`). Shared ones for a tap, a piece put down and a move that isn't allowed (`tap`, `put`, `wrong`) are for the games to use as each gets its sounds; the mrrp the owner picked for tape at first is gone (2026-11-14: they disliked it), and so is the sticky thhp after it (`tape` is silent everywhere since 2026-11-17, the owner's call, until something better turns up), and so is Moonpie's purr outside the Moonpie games (it's hers, and distinctive); the kit has a better hiss, chirp, meow, big yawn, water (`bubble`, `plip`, `splash`, `pour`, `dunk`), scratching, fabric rustle and crinkle, and a soft purr (`purr`, made, not recorded), all from the same day. The logic games' moments, picked once for the Cat Logic shelf (2026-11-14), are shared too, for any game that has them: something put in is `put`, a note or mark `mark` (a pencil tick), taking either out `erase` (a soft pop out), a rule broken where Auto check turns it red `wrong`, and a row, column or region done `line` (a soft note); a life lost (`life`, a soft bonk) plays from `G.lives`, Undo (`undo`, a rewind blip) from `G.history`, and Bookmark (`bookmark`, a page flick) and Return to Bookmark (`back`, a rewind swoosh, with no Undo blips for the steps it takes back) from `G.guesser`, so every game with those has them; `G.pad` plays its digits in and out, its notes, and `wrong` when a digit breaks a rule (the game says how many rules are broken, `broken`, and may name its own sound for a digit, `sound`). Objects and actions are to sound the same in every game (the owner, 2026-11-14): the shared sounds for them are in `games.js` (`spring`, `yarnLand`, `glassOver`, `offTable`, `catWakes`…), which a game's moment can point at with `G.sound.get`, each game keeping its own pick until the owner picks the objects again (the listening page); so do a letter typed (`key`), a word found (`word`, a run of notes), a piece in its place (`chime`) and a soft `swish`. Games get their own a shelf at a time: Cat Trails' seven, Cat Math's six, Cat Logic's eleven, On the Prowl's six (Pawlinko, Pounce Up, Tail Chase, Catlas, Cat Burglar and Bird Watch), Kitten Play's eight (Pounce on Three, Pawlette, Bubbles, Copycat, Cat Pile, Shelf Life, Wind Up and Mew Hue) and Swap-a-Pom have theirs (see their sections), and so do Cat Spells and Curious Cat: a key tap for a letter typed or changed (Keyboard Cat, Up a Tree, Kitten Caboodle), a run of notes for a word (and a basket of one toy in Toy Box), every game's low buzz for a word that isn't one or a block, ribbon or toy that can't go (in Twirl in place of a life's bonk), a magnet click (Fridge Magnets, each magnet linked in Junk Drawer), a soft swish for a toy pulled, a swap or a piece turned, a soft chime for a letter or piece in its place (Fish Bones, Purrmutation), Keyboard Cat's clatter as the cat knocks the keys off, Up a Tree's step up for a perch, L-M-N-O-Pom's soft tick to move or turn, Toy Box's fabric grab for a basket tipped (at 30%), Twirl's satin swish, Knock It Off's soft pad for a step and slide scrape for a push, Night Light's soft blink, If It Fits' cardboard tap, Cat Mat's dunk, Pick of the Pile's wood clack and two clacks for a pair, and Bed Hog's fabric rustle; a cat settling in (Night Light, If It Fits, Bed Hog) and a kitten waking (Kitten Caboodle) purr; L-M-N-O-Pom's pompom lands with a soft plop, and Knock It Off's things crash off the table. The big yawn is out everywhere (the owner hates it, 2026-11-14). A game can play a recording too: Moonpie's (`G.sound.clip`, for the Moonpie games) and the owner's CC0 finds from Freesound in `sounds/` (`G.sound.kit.rec(name, vol)`, 2026-11-15; `ARTWORK.md` lists them), each fetched once sound starts and only in a game that plays it. The owner's own volume and length for a sound are in `ADJ` (`G.sound.adjust`). If a sound fails it's logged to the console, never in the game's way.
- **Number colours:** games whose numbers each have a colour (Kit-Ten's tiles, Wind Up's balls of yarn, and since the same day High Perch's and Open the Door's squares and pads (`G.pad`'s `colours`), Treat Time's treats, Catwalk's badges, Scratch That's furniture and Hide & Seek's marker numbers) take them from `G.numbers`, one choice for every game (the owner's call, 2026-11-23). Style 1, the rainbow, is Kit-Ten's order round the colour wheel (lavender, periwinkle, pacific blue, sage, lime, gold, coral, brick, plum, spring blue), and everyone starts on it; Style 2 is Wind Up's own order from before (gold, spring blue, coral, pacific blue, plum, lime, periwinkle, brick, lavender, sage), which jumps about so neighbouring numbers differ in colour and lightness. Numbers sit on them in ink, or white on spring blue. The first time a player opens one of those games after this came in, once any walkthrough is done, a card asks "Which do you prefer?" with the line "Style 1 is more intuitive for some people; Style 2 makes it easier to tell numbers apart." and the two styles as a choice, each a strip of 1 to 10 in the same shape and numbers; it comes once, whatever they pick. The Settings page has the same choice in its own box (Number colours, under Every game). A change shows straight away, and in a game left open in another tab when it's next looked at. Kept in `catdoku.numbers.v1` (`{style, asked}`).
- **Settings page:** `settings/` has every game's settings on one page, reached from each game's ☰ Menu (All settings, which opens that game's shelf with its row lit; the menu keeps the game's own Settings as a shortcut) and from the front page (at the bottom). Since 2026-11-12 it's a grid of small icon buttons, each with its words small under it (the owner's design, to be less overwhelming). The top row, Every game, sets a thing for every game at once, and a tap goes round its three ways: Timer (Show · Hide · By game), Speed (Slow · Regular · By game, for the action games' Slower), Clutter (Regular · Reduced · By game: Reduced puts away Shelf Life's shelf, Junk Drawer's junk, Patches' fabric patterns, Cat Pile's cats, Bed Hog's cats and Twirl's twisty ribbons, and turns on Pick of the Pile's Dim stuck blocks and Junk Drawer's Dim used-up magnets, as dimming takes clutter away; `inv` in `games.js`), Auto check (Check · No check · By game) and Auto Notes (Notes · No notes · By game). Timer, Auto check and Auto Notes are lit green at their first way and faded at their second; Speed and Clutter, two ways neither of which is off, are blue at either (the owner, 2026-11-12: Slow or Regular, not Slow on or off); every one is dashed round at By game. Under it, a row for each shelf in the front page's order, with its games A to Z; tapping a shelf opens it and closes the others. Each game's row has its name and an icon for each setting it has, left to right: Timer, Speed, Clutter (one icon for Junk Drawer's two), Check, Notes, Sound (every game has it: On or Off; the top row's Sound is On · Off · By game, its six in one row of equal columns, two rows of three under 360 px wide), then its own (Auto grade, a heart; Rotation OK; Show sets), lit when on. The five columns' icons say their name and then which way the game has it: Timer Show or Hide, Speed Slow or Regular, Clutter Regular or Reduced, Check and Notes On or Off. A setting that can cost the gold paw (`paw` in `SETTINGS`: Auto grade in Catdoku, Toydoku and Fridge Magnets, as a life lost costs it; Under the Blanket's Auto Notes; Cat Pile's Turning) is drawn as the games draw such tools, in brick red with a small crossed-out paw, here and in the game's own menu (since 2026-11-12), and VoiceOver adds "costs the gold paw". A game's own choice shows its current words in blue and a tap goes round them: Numbers (Show numbers · Hide numbers, Toydoku's Show toys · Show numbers), Toe Beans' Match the Paw · Paw = 0, and Catlas's 4 Choices · Full List · Just Type. The rows' choices are kept in `catdoku.comfort.v2` (v1, until build .23, had Decorations' Show all and Auto check's and Auto Notes' Off first; it's turned round once into v2). Changing one game's (on the page, in the game's menu, or by tapping its clock) while a row is all one way moves the row to By game, and the other games keep theirs. The page doesn't run the games' scripts: `SETTINGS` in `games.js` says where each game keeps each setting (storage key and field, name, line, default, row), with `G.readSetting`/`G.writeSetting`. A setting that can cost a life (Auto grade, in Catdoku, Toydoku and Fridge Magnets), changed on the page while a puzzle's under way, shows "next game" under its icon and waits in `catdoku.pending.v1` until the game's next puzzle starts (`freshClock`).
- **Pieces:** `G.pieces` puts a game's plain tap and its marking tool side by side under the board, one lit at a time; with `many` and `face(k)` there are several plain pieces (Catdoku's Mark | Cat | Erase, Fair Play's Toy | Toy | Mark, where tapping still goes round from the toy picked: once for it, twice for the other). A double tap means the same across the marking games: it leaves a cat (Catdoku's cat, Hide & Seek's doodle).
- **Switches:** every on/off setting (Auto check, Auto Notes, Numbers, Slower…) is made by `G.switch({id, label, line, key, of, save, change})` in `games.js`, drawn in ☰ Menu's Settings with a short line under its name saying what it does (unless the name says it all: Timer has none), and kept with the game's own settings, as before. A switch with a `tip` shows it once, pointing at the switch, the first time it's turned on (never in the walkthrough).
- **Moving up and down:** three clean wins at a level move you up one and three misses move you down one, so nobody is carried to a level they can't win (the owner's call, 2026-11-12; in Night Light, Day Light, Open the Door! and Toe Beans first, and in nearly every other game with levels or sizes on 2026-11-20). A miss is a lost game, or a win or a puzzle that needed help: a hint, or a Restart after working on it, in the puzzles that can't be lost (Cat Math, Fair Play, High Perch, Scratch That, Cross Stitch, Bed Hog, Copycat, If It Fits, Junk Drawer, Kitten Caboodle, Knit Pick, Laser Pointer, Pawlette, Purrmutation, Shelf Life, Unravel, Up a Tree, Under the Blanket, which counts a peek, and Tape Trap), counted when it happens; and in the games that can be lost, counted once as the puzzle ends: running out of lives or moves, getting stuck, piling up, jamming or giving up, or winning with a hint or with what the game gives to keep going (an Audit, a letter filled in or a life lost in Fridge Magnets; more lives in Catdoku, Toydoku, Hide & Seek and Twirl; 5 more moves, swaps or a helper in Pounce, Swap-a-Pom, Bubbles and Toy Box; a swat in L-M-N-O-Pom; a shuffle in Pick of the Pile; going over the pots or swaps in Cat Mat and Fish Bones; getting stuck in Mew Hue even if Undo gets you going again). In Knock It Off only a hint counts (knocking off the wrong thing comes with a free Undo), and in Boing! too (its Restart is free). Patches, Sunbeams, Yarn, Tangle, Catwalk, Tunnel Run and Zoomies count a hint once the puzzle ends (Tunnel Run's tracks levels keep a tally of their own). Catdoku, Hide & Seek, Patches, Sunbeams, Yarn and Catwalk move by board size, and say so ("up to 7 × 7"). Zen and Endless are left out. The tally is kept by game on the device (`G.ladder`, `catdoku.ladder.v1`; `end` for a game that counts as the puzzle ends) and starts again when the level changes. The win card says where you are ("2 of 3 wins at level 4: one more moves you up"), its main button is Give me another!, and its level picker says Change the difficulty; a loss card that moves you down says so, and its button goes to the level below. The action games count a win and a loss (Tail Chase, Wind Up, Bird Watch, Pawlinko and Pounce Up; in Bird Watch wiping the windows is a miss too, and in Wind Up keeping going after the yarn piles over; in Pawlinko a hint). Moving Day counts a hint, or nothing left to do. Cat Burglar counts a round (four or five caught with no hints is a win) and Catlas a trip (8 on the first try, or 7 and a bonus, or all 10 found, with no hint or Restart), Catlas moving on and back through its rounds. Case Clawsed counts only a gold paw as a win. Every game with levels or sizes has it; Keyboard Cat and Pawstcards have no levels.
- **First size:** the games with a size picker open, for a new player, on a small board and a win moves them up: Catdoku and Patches on 6 × 6, Yarn and Sunbeams on 5 × 5 (since 2026-11-12; they opened on 8 × 8, 8 × 8, 6 × 6 and 7 × 7, where GoatCounter showed 53% to 68% of puzzles solved against 80% to 95% at the small size). ## Toy Box

A fifth game in `toybox/`, a cat-toy sorting game, starring Laya, a grey cat with brown eyes: she sits beside the title, lies on the rug inside the ball track watching the toys go round (fading away whenever a basket needs the space inside the loop, and back once it's gone), and struts off on the win card for a gold paw (her face is on the front-page icon too). Tap a wicker basket to tip its front toys onto a winding ball track; each toy drops into the first basket it reaches whose front toy matches, or the first empty basket, so timing a tap decides where toys go. Toys go once round before they can return to their own basket. If the two front toys differ, the one you tap is the one sent, and a toy can only leave once the whole row in front of it has gone. A toy alone in its row sits in the middle, so it reads as the front of the basket. Short tips pop up the first time two different front toys appear and the first time the track is nearly full. Fill a basket with eight of one toy and it's carried off, basket and all, so there are fewer baskets to work with as you go. Once every toy is put away, any baskets left empty are carried off too, so the floor behind the win card (and Admire Laya) is all tidy.
There are 27 levels, picked from a list at the top; they get harder up to level 21, and 22 to 27 bring in one new thing each (below): up to nine of the ten kinds of toy, a track that shrinks from 16 spaces to 5, more hidden toys and more split pairs. The track holds fewer toys as levels go on, and more toys start hidden in the back rows of baskets (a level hides none, or at least four toys of at least two kinds). From level 4 some matching pairs are dealt split up, so toys aren't always side by side with their match. If the track fills and nothing can find a basket, it jams. Every level is checked by a solver in a background Web Worker before it's dealt, so it can always be won with the right timing (the solver follows the same first-basket rule, and treats which rolling toy reaches an empty basket first as the player's choice). Toys are pictures from the shared toy library in `toys/`: ten kinds, one per palette colour, and each level picks one picture per colour (drawn stand-ins are used if a picture is missing). Each level has one of each helper: Shake a basket (rearranges one basket you pick), Shuffle all baskets (picks the mix that gives the most rolling toys somewhere to go), Lose a color (takes away every toy of one color) and Add a small basket (holds two toys). When the track jams, any helpers left are offered. Toys lets players leave out any toy pictures they don't like (kept on their device). Levels use up to nine colours, so at most one whole colour can be left out; a dealt level swaps it for a colour it doesn't use, which the solver doesn't mind since kinds are only labels to it. There's a timer with best times for each level, and a cat photo when you finish a level. New baskets deals another level of the same number, Restart sets out these baskets again (once you've started them, that costs the gold paw), and Share sends a link to these exact baskets.
Levels 22 to 27 (since 2026-11-15, the owner's picks) are level 16's baskets (nine kinds, seven spaces on the track) with one new thing each: 22 a pair of catnip mice (the toy mouse dyed sage green, with a leaf), which match any toy, so a mouse drops into the first basket with room and any toy can join a mouse; a basket finishes with eight of a kind, a mouse standing in for one, and then the rest of that kind are tidied away too, wherever they are, so none are stranded, and any mice left at the end go with the last basket; 23 two picture baskets, each with a badge of one toy on its back wall, which take only that toy, even when empty; 24 a fork in the track, just before the top stretch, with a bridge inside the loop past the three top baskets' mouths: each toy that reaches the fork goes the other way from the one before (an arrow shows which way the next goes), and a toy on the bridge can't drop into those baskets; 25 a basket on wheels, which rolls into the spot of each basket that's carried off; 26 gift-wrapped toys (about three in ten, in plum paper with a gold bow), which show what they are only once they land in a basket; and 27 one more basket than there are spots, the last waiting off the floor ("1 basket waiting") until a basket's carried off, when it slides into that spot. Each gets a one-step walkthrough the first time. The solver knows all six (`takes`, `placeToy`, `afterPlace`, and baskets keep their spot, so the basket on wheels and the waiting basket change the order toys reach them); with the fork it follows the toys it sends out, alternating, but not toys still going round, so a level 24 is checked a little more loosely than the rest. Share links carry the new things (`o`, `m`, `f`, `w`; kinds are written in hex, so a catnip mouse is `a`).

## Twirl

A seventh game in `twirl/`, starring Bugsy, a fluffy brown and white cat who wants to nap on the mat: Bugsy curls up beside the title, peeks over the top corner of the mat while you play, and dozes in a box on the win card for a gold paw (Bugsy is also on the front-page icon). Satin ribbons cover the whole mat; tap one and it slides off the way its pointed end points, following its own path, but only if nothing is in the way. Hint lights a ribbon that's free to go. A blocked tap costs a life, as in Catdoku: three lives, and running out offers a restart, new ribbons, or three more lives (up to nine). There are 28 levels, plus a level 0 with a 9 × 14 mat where the ribbons show twice as big as on level 1, and Endless; levels 1 to 20 run from an 18 × 27 mat of shorter ribbons to a 28 × 42 mat of long, winding ones, and levels 21 to 28 bring in one new thing each (below); three levels finished on the first three lives move you up, and three that needed more lives, a hint or a restart move you down (Moving up and down). Big mats zoom (pinch, the + and − under the mat's corner or on the keyboard, or Ctrl/⌘ + scroll; Fit shows the whole mat again) and you drag to look around. They're the shared zoom buttons (`G.zoom`), on a line under the mat's corner rather than on it, since the mat at Fit can't be moved from under them, with Hint in the middle of that line (decided 2026-11-08, to take less room); every game that zooms or goes full screen has them in the same place. The first of them (or F) goes full screen: the mat takes all the room the screen has, laid sideways when its squares come out bigger that way (on a laptop, 28 × 42 squares go from about 13 pixels to 21), with the lives above, zoom under its corner, Hint on that line, and Restart below (on a phone held sideways, in a column beside it). Taps, the arrow keys and the walkthrough follow the mat round. New ribbons lays out another mat at the same level, and Share sends a link to this mat (colours are picked afresh for whoever opens it).
Past level 20 (since 2026-11-15, the owner's picks), ribbons as long and winding as level 20's, on mats a little smaller, with one new thing a level: at 21 (22 × 33) some ribbons are pointed at both ends, and go from the end nearer the tap (from the middle square, whichever end is free); at 22 (24 × 36) ribbons cross over and under, 24 crossings a mat, and the one underneath can't go until the one on top has gone (it gives a shake, "Another ribbon lies across it", and costs a life); at 23 (26 × 39) pairs of ribbons lying side by side are tied together with a little cream bow, 14 pairs a mat, and go together, once both are free (each may pass through the other); and at 24 (28 × 42) all three. The first time each turns up it gets a walkthrough step of its own. Crossings are made by cutting the two ribbons either side of a straight run of another and joining a piece of each across it; nothing is cut next to another crossing, and a crossing is never at the end of a ribbon. A ribbon on top of a crossing is always flat (a twist would narrow to nothing just there), with a soft shadow under it at the crossing. Knots are tied one pair at a time, each kept only if the mat can still be cleared. The rules are plain functions shared with the worker (`goes`, `stopOf`, `playOrder`): a ribbon going never stops another, so playing every free ribbon as soon as it's free clears any mat that can be cleared, and every mat is checked that way before it's dealt; that order, turned round, is also the order the ribbons are drawn in, so the one on top of a crossing is always drawn last. Hint knows all three: it lights only the free half of a ribbon pointed both ways, and both ribbons of a knot. Share links carry them too (`b`, `c` and `k`), and a shared mat is checked before it opens. Then four more (2026-11-15, the owner's ideas): 25 is a mat bigger than the screen, 40 × 60, that opens zoomed in (its squares 26 pixels), so you scroll round it and keep in mind what's at each height, and Fit zooms all the way out to show the whole mat, tiny (its ribbons little more than 1-point lines on a phone; the owner's call, 2026-11-16: until then it never zoomed out past 26 pixels and had no Fit); 26 is every ribbon in one colour, all flat or all twisty, so you follow them by shape; 27 is thin ribbons, drawn as pointed lines (always flat); and 28 is very long ribbons, with crossings and knots, so it takes concentration to keep track of which is which, and so which is free: about 30 to a 28 × 42 mat, the longest 120 to 180 squares, and four fifths of the mat in ribbons 30 squares or longer, with 20 crossings and 6 knots, every ribbon a shade of one of the close colourways (Lavender, Ocean, Moss…; neighbours as different as the family allows), so colour doesn't tell them apart. Its paths wind with few straight runs, and after the crossings are made a ribbon left short is joined end to end with a neighbour (never one it crosses); the solver still cuts some to keep the mat clearable, so eight mats are made and the one with the fewest ribbons is kept. Level 25 was tiny ribbons (2 to 4 squares long, about 400 on a 28 × 42 mat) until the owner took it out on 2026-11-16, so levels 26 to 29 became 25 to 28: a save, the level you're up to and best times are moved to the new numbers when the page opens (a tiny-ribbons mat part-played is let go), a share link carries how many levels there were (`L`) so one from before is moved as it opens (a tiny-ribbons mat opens as level 24's), and GoatCounter's counts for levels 25 to 29 before that day are the old levels.
Endless (∞ at the end of the level list, since 2026-11-15) is a mat that keeps unrolling: 18 across and 27 down, made of bands 9 rows deep. No ribbon in it points down, so nothing waits on anything below it, and a band added at the bottom never blocks what's above; each band is made to clear on its own, so the whole mat always can. When the top band is clear it rolls away (the mat slides up) and a new band comes in at the bottom. Lives work as on a level (three, and the revives), and running out ends the run, with New run or Back to the levels (the level you were up to is kept while you play Endless). The score is ribbons cleared, with your best kept on this device (`catdoku.twirl.endless.v1`); a run can't be shared, and a run part-way through is kept like a level.
Every ribbon wears its own colourway from `colorways.js` (shared with Yarn), chosen to stand apart from the ribbons it touches. Early levels have solid ribbons in a mix of flat and twisty textures, which makes them easy to tell apart; later levels shade each ribbon through its colourway (ombré), and from level 13 each board is all twisty or all flat (picked at random), so you follow ribbons by shape. A Twisty ribbons toggle (kept on the device, and in the Reduce visual clutter row on the Settings page) draws every ribbon flat on every level for anyone who'd rather. Grid (off at first, kept on the device and on the Settings page; the owner's idea, 2026-11-16) draws faint lines round every square of the mat, one screen pixel wide, under the ribbons, to see which way a ribbon has to go and what's in its way. Ribbons come as coils, spirals, staircases, zigzags and winding paths.
Mats are made by covering every square with ribbons, then pointing each one and playing the board through: when nothing can move, a ribbon is turned round (or, as a last resort, cut in two) so something can, choosing the one that opens up latest so few moves are open at once. The playthrough is a solution, and the board is played through again to be sure, so every mat can be cleared.

## Toe Beans

An eighth game in `toebeans/`, a numbers puzzle on a cat's paw. Six numbers sit in tiles above the paw; the paw's four toe beans are + − × ÷ and its big pad shows the goal. Tap a number, a toe bean, then another number: the two combine and the answer takes the second number's place. Make the goal exactly (you don't have to use every number) and every pad on the paw fills at once in gold: a golden paw.
There are 10 levels; higher ones have bigger goals that need more steps (one at level 1, where two of the numbers make the goal, two at level 2, five at level 10; level 1 needed two steps until 2026-11-12, when GoatCounter showed 28 starts there and 13 solves, and Reverse still starts at two). ## Knock It Off

A ninth game in `knockitoff/`: Fred, its mascot (a slender white cat with a tabby cap, a tabby patch behind one shoulder and a sky-blue collar, drawn from above on the table), pushes things off a tabletop. Walk into a thing to push it one square; push it past the edge and it falls off with a crash. The cat can only push, never pull, only one thing at a time, and never steps off the table. Mugs, glasses, succulents, vases, phones and lamps push one square; balls of yarn and pencils roll until they hit something; lemons (cats can't stand citrus) and piles of books won't budge; grey walls along a side stop things falling there. Your food bowl has to stay on the table, so knocking it off ("Not that one!") offers Undo or Restart. A lit candle pushes one square too, but it mustn't end up next to a pile of books ("Fire!"), and once it falls off you have 6 moves to knock off a glass of water or a vase to put the fire out ("The fire spread!"); a flame by the table and a count beside the moves show the fire while it burns. Patches of catnip on the table make the cat roll about: stepping onto one counts as 3 moves, so the shortest route isn't always the obvious one (things can sit on catnip; only the cat is slowed). Strips of tape are worse: the cat won't step on them at all. Things can be pushed onto tape and rolling things stop on it, but anything on tape is out of the cat's reach. A roll of paper towel has to go too: pushed the way its loose end points, it moves a square and lays paper on the squares it leaves and lands on; pushed back, it rolls like yarn and winds its paper up again (paper stuck on tape tears off and stays); pushed sideways, its paper tears off and stays. Paper over tape is safe for the cat to walk on, and rolling things stop on paper but can still be pushed. What's what, in ☰ Menu, opens a card with the key to this table: what to knock off, what to protect and what won't budge. It isn't on the page, since working it out is part of the fun, and How to play shows each new thing the first time it's on a table.
Swipe or use the arrow keys (or WASD) to step, or tap a square and the cat walks there the shortest way. Undo is free; Restart costs the gold paw. The first time you play, a spotlight walks through the table; and the first time each new kind of thing turns up (lemons and books, rolling things, walls, the food bowl, new table shapes, the candle, catnip, tape, paper towels), it gets a one-line spotlight of its own. Hint shows the next step on one of the shortest ways to clear the table, and after each move a note says if something has got stuck for good.
There are 20 levels, from a 5 × 5 table with three things up to 8 × 8 tables with lemons, books, walls, rolling things, a food bowl and an L- or U-shaped table. Something new arrives at each of the first nine levels: lemons and books at level 2, rolling things at 3, walls at 4, the food bowl at 5, odd-shaped tables at 6, the candle at 7, catnip at 8, tape at 9 and the paper towel roll at 10; after that the tables grow and mix everything, with a candle or a paper towel roll on alternate levels, catnip and one or two strips of tape. Tables are made ahead of time: `knockitoff/tools/make-tables.js` (run with Node) deals 600 tables per level with the game's own rules from `knockitoff/engine.js`, counts the traps on each (pushes from the start that leave the table impossible to clear), and keeps the 300 with the most traps in `knockitoff/tables/NN.json`. Levels 1 and 2 keep the 300 with the fewest traps instead (none at all, as dealt on 2026-11-12, and a move or two shorter), so no first push on a first table can leave it impossible to clear: GoatCounter showed 27 starts and 17 clears at level 1, and 18 and 7 at level 2. The game picks one you haven't played yet on that device, so a table is ready at once; if a level's file can't be loaded it deals one in a background Web Worker instead. Every table can be cleared and needs at least a set number of moves for its level; the same search finds the fewest moves possible. Run the tool again after changing the rules or the levels. It searches push by push (the cat walks the shortest way between pushes), skips squares a thing could never be pushed off from, and keeps count of the fire's moves. Three tables cleared without a hint move you up a level and three with one move you down (Moving up and down); clearing it in the fewest moves possible without a hint earns a gold paw. There's a timer with best times for each level, a share link, and a cat photo when you clear a table; a gold paw shows Fred batting a toy mouse instead. Fred also sits beside the title and is the cat on the front-page icon.

## Pawlette

An eleventh game in `pawlette/` (first called Nip Trip; `niptrip/` now forwards there, and the napping cat is still kept under its old `niptrip` name), a cat-themed colour-matching game, on the Kitten Play shelf (where it started; on Curious Cat from 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-12, then back, the owner's call). Since November 2026 it's Mix to order: dye a skein of yarn to match one of Yarn's colourways (`colorways.js`), a colour at a time.

Along the top of the bench, the colourway runs as a strand of yarn blending through all its colours, with its name under it and a ring on each colour you're matching (gold on the one you're mixing for); just below it is your skein, undyed cream until you dye it. Under them sit the colour to match and your mix, then the paint pots: red, yellow and blue (with Mew Hue's marks: a bar down, a bar across and a ring), white (a dashed outline) and, on levels 4 and 5, black (a dot). Tap a pot to drip a drop into the cup. Paint mixes like paint: the red-yellow-blue part is blended between the corners of the paint cube (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple and brown), then white lightens it and black darkens it, so red and yellow make orange, opposites make browns, and white makes pastels. There's no score while you mix: you judge by eye. Dye it dyes that colour with your mix, any time; the target and your mix stay on screen while a word pops up big over the bench and bounces away (Perfect! in gold, So close!, Nice! or Not quite) with, for a miss, what would have matched better ("A little more red", "Less white"): the one drop more or less of a pot that brings it closest. The dyed colour fills its ring and your skein. Empty the cup and Dye it sit below the bench, not in it. Empty the cup starts the colour again; Restart starts the whole strand again (costing the gold paw), and once anything's dyed it asks first and offers Empty the cup instead.

A skein's colours can be anywhere along the strand: its rings go at random points, in order and never overlapping, and the colour at each is the strand's own blend there (its listed colours spread evenly along it). A colourway you've dyed in your last 10 skeins isn't picked again while there's another to choose (kept on the device). Each colour to match is the mixable colour nearest that point's colour, found among every mix of up to the level's drops (fewest drops kept for each colour, no two the same on one skein), so a perfect match is always possible; the paint can't reach every colour of every colourway (the coolest blues and some pastels), so a skein looks like its colourway, a touch softer. Five levels: 3 colours in up to 4 drops; 4 in up to 6; 5 in up to 8; 5 with black; and 6 to 8 with black. A few colourway names are in two of Yarn's sets with different colours, and both come up. Hint marks the pot to drip next on the way to the fewest-drop recipe, or the pot there's too much of; asked again it gives the whole recipe, and with a perfect mix in the cup it says to dye it. Three with a hint or Restart at a level move you down and three clean wins move you up (Moving up and down); every colour perfect, in the fewest drops, with no hint or Restart, earns the gold paw, and the finished skein winds into a ball on the win card. A curled-up cat from Sunbeams naps on the corner of the bench and purrs at a perfect match or a pat. Timer and best times for each level; progress is saved. Share sends an invitation with a link.

Pawlette used to have two modes. **Zen** was a calm board of paint blocks to mix with no goal, over a catnip haze that breathed and took on the last colour made; it became Mix to order (the breathing, colour-changing glow is saved for something later, `IDEAS.md`). **Swipe** became its own game, Mew Hue (below), in November 2026; anyone whose last Pawlette game was Swipe is told once where it went.

Sound (2026-11-14, the owner's picks; `G.sound`, on unless Settings turns it off): a plip for each drop, a pour as the cup's emptied, a dunk at Dye it, then a sparkle for Perfect!, two notes up for So close! or Nice!, and a soft bonk for Not quite; the napping cat purrs a soft purr at a perfect match, a pat or the last colour (Moonpie's own purr until the owner kept hers for the Moonpie games). The drop, pour and dunk are made of little rising bubbles.

## Open the Door!

A sixteenth game in `opendoor/`, a cat-themed KenKen: the board is a house, and thick walls split it into rooms. Fill every square with a number from 1 to N so that every row and column has each number once, and each room's numbers make its sign: 7+ adds up to 7, 2− is a difference of 2, 12× multiplies to 12 and 3÷ divides to 3 (a room of one square just shows its number). − and ÷ work either way round (the bigger number minus or divided by the smaller), and a number can repeat in a room as long as it isn't twice in one row or column; the walkthrough says so on a room of each kind when the puzzle has one. Cats hate shut doors, so every room has one, hung in one of its walls: get the room right and its door swings open and its floor lights up. Open every door and a cat runs right across the house, then the finishing card. Tap a square, then a number (or type it; arrow keys move); tap the number again or the eraser to take it away. Hold a number down and it sticks, as Toydoku's toys do: each square tapped takes it, until it's held again (`G.pad`, so High Perch and Window Ledges do the same). Notes jots down numbers a square might be, and Auto Notes 🔢 (a switch in the settings, off at first) puts every number its row and column leave open in every empty square as notes, to cross off, at once and on every new puzzle; a number put down comes off the notes in its row and column, one taken away goes back on them, and turning it off takes every note away (Undo puts it back). A number already in its row or column turns red, and a full room whose numbers don't make its sign shows its sign in red and rattles its door. Puzzles are a random full grid cut into rooms of one to four squares, each given a sign; while a solver finds a second answer, a room where the two differ gets a new sign or is split, and it's checked again, so every puzzle has exactly one answer. On levels 1 to 7, a room where the solver gets stuck and would have to guess is changed the same way, so those puzzles can be worked out without guessing. They're made in a background Web Worker. There are 8 levels: a 4 × 4 with + only and rooms of one or two squares (a first house: since 2026-11-12, when GoatCounter showed 27 starts at level 1 and 11 solves; before that it had − and rooms of three, and it never gives away more than four numbers), a 5 × 5 with + and − and rooms of up to three, a 5 × 5 that brings in ×, then 5 × 5 to 9 × 9 with all four signs and rooms of up to four; the 9 × 9 may need a guess. One new sign comes in a level (since 2026-11-12, the owner's "one new move a level"): − at level 2, × at 3 and ÷ at 4, and every house at those levels has at least two rooms with its new sign. The first time a sign turns up on a device, one of its rooms gets a spotlight of its own saying what it means ("20×: these multiply to 20"); + is in the walkthrough. Until then there were 7 levels, with × and ÷ arriving together at level 3, so levels 3 to 7 became 4 to 8: saves, best times, the moving-up tally and older share links are moved to the new numbers, and GoatCounter's counts for level 3 and up mean the old levels before that day. Hint points out a number that's wrong, if there is one, and otherwise glows the square to work out next and says why, then (pressed again) its number, for you to put in. Since 2026-11-23 it follows the steps every house is checked with (`nextStep`), simplest first: its row and column leave one number; it's the only place left in its row or column for a number; or, of all the ways its room can make its sign, only one number fits there (a room on its own, or the last square of a room, said as such); when it takes the rooms' ways ruling numbers out first, it says so, and on a 9 × 9 with no sure step it says the house may need a guess (until then it glowed a square in the room nearest full, and gave a reason only when one of the first two showed); a hint costs the gold paw and is a miss (Moving up and down). Three solved without a hint or Clear move you up and three with one move you down (Moving up and down); solving one with no hint or Clear earns a gold paw (Undo is free). The ÷ in a sign is drawn (two clear dots and a bar), as the font's is so small it reads as a +. There's a timer with best times for each level and a share link. Once every door's open, Hide numbers (with the tools) takes the numbers off the board so the sleeping cats and open doors show, and Show numbers puts them back; the choice is remembered on the device.

A hint costs the gold paw, as do 5 more swaps (offered once on the Out of swaps card) and a Restart once the tray's started; a win without them earns the gold paw. Three wins without them at a level move you up, and three trays at a level with one, or run out of swaps, move you down (Moving up and down). Restart puts this tray back as it was dealt, New tray deals another, and Share sends a link to the tray as dealt (the level and seed). There's a timer with best times for each level, a first-visit walkthrough with your first swap played for real, keys for everything, and a VoiceOver description of the tray.

## Moving Day

A fifty-ninth game in `movingday/`, a cat-themed merge game (like Merge Mansion, Travel Town or Gossip Harbor, without their energy timers or story), on the Kitten Play shelf: you've just moved in, the room is stacked with moving boxes, and the cats want their things. The room is 7 × 7 squares of floor in a cardboard-brown frame, most of them under Copycat's boxes (`boxes.js`), some two high. In the middle are a treat jar and a toy basket. Tap one for something small (a tap each, from the level's taps on the status line); drag one thing onto another just like it and the two make the next one along, where you let go; a merge beside a box opens it (a two-high box needs two). Tap a thing, then a square, does the same as a drag. Old boxes hold toys (never food: it would have gone off), and a few toys lie under cobwebs, stuck until one the same is merged onto them. Along the top, each cat (one of eight general cats sitting up) shows what it wants beside it; when you have it, its card glows green with "Tap to give" under it, and a tap hands it over. Give every cat what it wants to win.

The two kinds of thing, seven steps each, are listed under the game (What's what), and picking one up names it and what two make: from the treat jar, kibble, a fish treat (Treat Time's biscuit, now in `treats.js`), a bowl of food (Knock It Off's, from `tablethings.js`), a tin of sardines, a salmon dinner, a fish feast and a fish birthday cake; from the toy basket, a scrap of yarn (drawn as the Yarn game draws its strand), a ball of yarn (`yarnBall` in `colorways.js`), a pompom (Spelling String's photo, tinted sage), the toy mouse and the toy fish (`toys/`), a knitted cat bed and a yarn-wrapped cat tree. The Numbers switch puts each thing's step on it.

There are 12 levels, one new thing each from level 4: levels 1 to 3 are gentle (open floor round the jar and basket, plenty of taps); 4 starts with only the squares beside them free, so you have to unbox for room; 5 has two-high boxes near the middle; 6 a cat who wants two things; 7 the jar and basket now and then giving the other kind; 8 to 11 step 6 and more cats; 12 step 7. Each level has fewer spare taps and fewer toys in boxes than the one before. A room is laid out at random from a seed (boxes, what's in them, cobwebs), then played by a plain player (give what it can, merge pairs it needs, else tap for what's short; with no room, merge anything alike); a room it can't finish is dealt again, so every room can be won, and the gold paw is for keeping three-quarters of the taps it kept. Rooms are made in the background Web Worker. Out of taps with the orders out of reach, or a full floor with nothing to merge or give, ends the room (Undo, Restart, New room).

Hint shows what to do next: a cat you can give to (its card and the thing in gold), then a pair to merge (a webbed one first), then the jar or basket for what's short. A hint costs the gold paw and is a miss, as is a room with nothing left to do (Moving up and down). Undo takes back a tap, merge, move or gift (the jar's mixed taps included); Restart puts the room back as it was dealt. On a keyboard, the arrows move a cursor over the room, Space or Enter taps, 1 to 4 give to a cat and Escape lets go. The timer, best times by level, Share (the level and seed) and the walkthrough (the cats, a tap and a merge on the real room, giving, boxes and cobwebs) work as in the other games. Full screen isn't needed: the room is square and fills a phone's width. Built from a playable preview with the owner (2026-11-19 and 20); see `IDEAS.md`.

## Tape Trap

A sixtieth game in `tapetrap/`, in beta (not on the front page yet), on the Cat Logic shelf: a new kind of logic puzzle (Cat Trap in `IDEAS.md`), starring Marmalade (`marmalade-1` sits beside the title, and `marmalade-5` lies stretched out asleep on the gold-paw card). A wooden floor is seen from above, with cats sitting on some of its squares: Marmalade and his friends Gus, Peanut and Willow, taking turns (their faces, `marmalade-7`, `gus-1`, `peanut-1` and `willow-7`). Each cat shows how many steps away its square of masking tape is, counting up, down, left and right. Lay one tape square for every cat: each cat walks to its nearest tape and sits in it, so that tape must be exactly its number of steps away, with no other tape nearer or just as near, and no two cats may want the same one. A roll of tape at the end of every row and column counts the tape squares in it.

Tap a square to lay tape or take it up; hold it, right-click it, or use Dot to mark a square that stays bare. Tap a cat to see its reach: the squares nearer than its number are shaded (no tape can go there) and those at its number are ringed. A cat's badge turns green once it has its own tape, and red when something's wrong, with the line under the board saying what (a nearer tape, two just as near, or two cats wanting the same one); a roll turns green when its line has the right amount of tape and red when it has too much. When every cat is settled, each walks along to its tape and sits in it.

Floors are made in a background Web Worker: the tape is scattered at random, each tape's cat is put on a square whose nearest tape is that one and no other as near, the counts are added, and the floor is kept if the solver finds exactly one answer (about three in four are; a floor takes a few milliseconds even at the top level). There are 11 levels, from a 4 × 4 floor with 3 cats to 10 × 10 with 16; the higher levels lean towards cats further from their tape, though floors with far cats more often have more than one answer, so most cats are 1 or 2 steps away.

Hint shows a mistake first, in red (tape where none goes, or a dot where tape goes). Otherwise it lights up, in gold, the cat with the fewest squares left for its tape and those squares; asked again, it shows the one square. A hint costs the gold paw, as Restart does, and three floors with either at a level move you down, as three clean ones move you up (Moving up and down); Undo is free. There's a timer with best times for each level, a share link (the cats, their numbers and the answer, with your tape if you like), a first-visit walkthrough, and the arrow keys with Space to lay tape and X or . for a dot.

## Boing!

A sixty-first game in `boing/`, in beta (not on the front page yet), on the Curious Cat shelf: a cat-themed spring-placing puzzle, after The Incredible Machine, starring Ferguson (a fluffy black cat with yellow eyes: `ferguson-sit` beside the title, `ferguson-basket` curled up in the basket the ball has to land in, and `ferguson-leap` on the gold-paw card). A room is seen from the side: a toy ball rolls off a shelf at the top left (one of the toy library's bouncy balls for each room: the tennis ball, the red, teal or yellow wiffle ball, the bell ball, the lattice ball, the felt rainbow ball or the polka-dot ball), and Ferguson waits in a basket on a cupboard. Stand the springs from the tray in the room, turn them, and press Go to watch the ball bounce; a dotted trail shows where it went last time. When it drops into the basket, Ferguson's caught it.

Springs stand only on something: the floor, or the top of a box, a chest of drawers, a pile of books or a shelf on the wall (the owner, 2026-11-13: springs in the air weren't intuitive). Drag one in from the tray (or tap it there and then the room) and it drops onto whatever's under it, anywhere along it in steps of 5 units; while it's held, a see-through spring shows where it would land, with a red ring where it can't stand (against furniture, or too close to another spring). On a phone it's held a little above the finger. Drag a placed spring to nudge it along or move it, drag it off the room to take it back, and tap it to turn it: standing, leaning right, lying flat, leaning left. Dotted lines along the floor and furniture show where springs can stand. The springs are cut from the owner's spring sheet (Toy library, below), about twice the ball's size.

The ball is worked out in fixed steps, the same every time (`fly` in `boing/index.html`, used by the room maker, Hint and Go alike): gravity, a little bounce off the floor, walls and furniture, and rolling to a stop. A coil is a springy pad at the end it points to: the ball bounces off that end like a mirror, thrown back along the coil at least its spring's speed and keeping most of its speed across it, so where and at what angle it lands matters (the owner's notes, 2026-11-13); hit on its side, a coil is just something solid. Lying flat, a coil is a springy floor that throws the ball back as high as it fell; a ring (end-on) is a round bumper that sends it off the way it bounced, a little faster.

Rooms are built backwards from an answer, in a Web Worker (the next level's is made while you play): each spring goes where the ball comes down onto it, a little off its middle, with something put under it to stand on if nothing's there (a box, drawers or books near the floor, a wall shelf higher up); the basket goes where the ball comes down at the end, on a cupboard; other furniture goes where the ball doesn't, as false leads. A room is kept only if the ball misses with no springs and with any one spring left out, and each coil of the answer still works a step or two along (a ring's bounce depends on just where it's hit, so a ring needn't); each answer coil works across about 25 units. Eight levels, one new thing each: one standing spring, one to turn, two springs, a ring, a coil lying flat, then three springs, with more furniture in the way. Every room is made in under half a second.

Hint works from the answer: first a spring that isn't where the answer has one (in red), then where one goes (a gold ring); asked again, it says which spring and which way it's turned. A spring within two steps of the answer's counts as there. Three rooms with a hint at a level move you down and three without move you up (Moving up and down; Restart is free, as the goes still count); a hint costs the gold paw, which is for catching the ball in three goes or fewer with no hint. Restart puts every spring back in the tray; it doesn't cost the gold paw, since the goes still count. New room deals another, Share sends a link to the room as dealt, and there's a timer with best times for each level, a first-visit walkthrough (turning a spring on the real floor, then putting it back), keys for everything and a VoiceOver description of the room. Sounds: the shared spring boing when the ball meets a coil, a quicker, higher boing off a ring, a soft thump when it hits anything else hard, the shared sproing when a spring is put down, and Ferguson's chirp-purr when it lands.

## Kit-Ten

A sixty-second game in `kitten/`, on the Kitten Play shelf (it's a merge game more than a maths one, the owner, 2026-11-22; it was on Cat Math in beta), on the front page from 2026-11-22: a Make Ten game, the owner's own favourite with the ads taken out (there's no cat reason for it; the name is the joke). Each game deals one of four kittens, Gus, Peanut, Marmalade or Willow (from the game's seed, so a shared game has the same one; `KITTENS` in the page), who grows up beside the title as the biggest tile climbs: a kitten sitting, then playing from a 160, then grown up from a 1,280 (the owner's wish for a kitten growing up, and more than one cat, 2026-11-22). Each step up pops the new picture in, says "Gus is growing up!" (or "all grown up!") and plays a kitten's chirp (`chirp-01`; grown up is the shared cat cheer). Undo takes the step back with the join. A join of 40 or more has the kitten rise over the tile, or once grown up just their face. The card at the end shows them as they got, pleased with themselves after a new best or asleep otherwise, and says whether they grew up or what tile it takes. A board of 5 × 5 number tiles. Drag across touching tiles (sideways, up and down, or corner to corner), none twice: small numbers (1 to 9) that add up to exactly 10 make a 10, and from 10 up a tile joins only a matching one (10 and 10 make 20, 20 and 20 make 40…). The rules are the owner's (2026-11-22: any run of small numbers to 10, and tens only with their match). A running total sits over the last tile while you drag, gold when it will join; let go and the tiles join on the last one, the rest clear, each column falls and new tiles drop in at the top. Or tap tile after tile and tap the last one again. A chain that won't join says why in the message line. The score goes up by each new tile's value, and the best is kept on the device (`catdoku.kitten.v1`). The game ends when nothing on the board will join, on a card with the score, the joins, the biggest tile and the best, and New game, Undo, a look at the board, Share and Front page.

There are no levels, timer or gold paw: it's endless, played for a best score, and every Hint and tool is free (`G.pawLost(() => true)`, so none shows the gold paw's red). New tiles are 1 to 6, all alike, with now and then a 7 (4 in 100), an 8 (2 in 100) or a 9 (1 in 100); until 2026-11-22 a 7 was one in 25 and there were never 8s or 9s, as on the owner's starting board in Make Ten, and the owner asked for them, and the k-th tile to drop is the same whatever's been played (from the game's seed), so a shared game is the same game. Before that, with any chain to 10, 20, 40… and tiles 1 to 9, a game never ended (3,000 joins in every simulated game), and a bar of moves was tried; pairing the tens and the small tiles made it end by itself. Following Hint every time (40 games, 2026-11-22), a game lasted a median of 447 joins and 15,920 points, with a biggest tile of 1,280 (27 of 40 reached 1,280, so the kitten grew up), before the 8s and 9s; with them, 228 to 299 joins and a biggest tile of 640, and 13 or 14 of 40 reaching 1,280. The owner's own way does far better: use a 6 to 9 whenever one can join (they need particular partners, so they block the board), and keep the big tiles low and to the left, where nothing under them clears, by ending joins low (1,078 joins, 43,300 points, a biggest tile of 2,560 and 35 of 40 growing up; ending small joins high instead, 240 joins). The owner's other rules: only tens on the bottom row (a small number there can only leave by being joined, and it splits the tens beside it), and the big tiles built up as a pyramid towards the bottom middle, fed by three highways (a middle square has five neighbours, a corner three). Hint plays all of it since the same day: of every join it keeps to those that leave no more small numbers on the bottom row, then to those that use a 6 to 9 if any can (saying "Use the 7 while it has partners"), then the best one join ahead, with a pair of tens landing low and towards the middle counting for more. Following it, a game now lasts a median of 2,106 joins and 97,950 points, with a biggest tile of 5,120; 39 of 40 grow up, 29 reach 5,120, and 7 were still going at 4,000 joins (6 to 9 first alone: 1,142 joins and 2,560; with the bottom-row rule too, 1,395); a player picking joins at random is stuck within about 20. Each number has its own colour, in the player's number colours (Number colours, under Across every game): in Style 1 (the owner's chart, 2026-11-22, and everyone's to start with) 1 to 9 are the site's palette from lavender through periwinkle, pacific blue, sage, lime, gold, coral and brick to plum, in ink, and 10 solid spring blue, in white; Style 2 has Wind Up's order instead, with 2 on spring blue in white. From 20 up the same order comes again at 70% (tinted towards white), in ink, 20 taking 1's colour and 10,240 taking 10's, then round again. The numbers are big and bold, so 3 to 1 is enough contrast; the lowest are on brick (3.2) and plum (3.0). Thousands are set off by a little space, not a comma ("1 280", "123 456"), as German readers expect a point there (the owner, 2026-11-22, with German translation ahead): a set gap of a fifth of a letter on the tiles, the score and the running total, where the font draws a narrow space too thin to see, and a narrow no-break space in the sentences on the cards and notes. GoatCounter's score bands keep their commas.

Hint plays the owner's way (above): of the joins that keep the bottom row all tens and use a 6 to 9 if any can, it looks one join ahead: of every join on the board (a chain over the same squares ending on the same one is counted once), the one whose board after, new tiles and all, has the most joins and the fewest tens with no match beside them, a pair of tens counting for a little more; it lights the tiles in gold and says the sum. Undo, three a game (a count on the button, which goes once they're used), takes back a join, including the one that ended the game; Restart puts back the game as it was dealt, with three undos again. On a keyboard the arrows move a cursor, Space or Enter adds the tile under it to the chain (or, on the chain's last tile, joins it) and Escape lets go. The walkthrough has you make a real 10 on your own board and puts it back afterwards, then shows that diagonals count, that tens pair up, and that the game ends when nothing joins.

## Toy library

`toys/` holds 32 cat toy pictures for any game to use: 256 × 256 WebP with a clear background, named by what they are (`wiffle-red`, `fish`, `cork-paw`…). The ones in `toys/` are recoloured to the games' palette; `toys/original/` has the same toys in their first colours, and `toys/earlier/` an earlier set. `toys/sheets/` keeps uncut sheets for later: `springs.webp` (1312 × 1199) is the owner's sheet of coil springs, ten rows in Wind Up's yarn colours in the same order (gold, slate, coral, teal, plum, lime, periwinkle, brick, lavender, green) and nine poses across (standing, tilted both ways, end-on, lying flat, bent into an arc). Boing! (in beta) uses four of its poses (standing, tilted, end-on and lying flat) in eight of its colours, cut out at 96 pixels into `boing/springs/`; they show clean on Boing!'s pale walls. It isn't cut into toys for the library yet: its background is only partly clear, with coloured fringes round each spring to clean off first. `springs-grey.webp` (2172 × 724) is the same nine poses once, in neutral grey on a clear background with no fringes, each spring a separate piece 170 to 470 pixels across, made to be tinted to any palette colour as Spelling String tints its pompom: lighten the grey (about × 1.45, since it's mid-grey) and multiply by the colour (× 1.22). Tried that way, gold, slate and coral came out matching the palette.
